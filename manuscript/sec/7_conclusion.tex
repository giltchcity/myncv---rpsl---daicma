\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a method for carrying a dense dynamic scene map into a later
independently started deployment. Object states are stored with their surfaces
and histories, then refined or ended using new geometric evidence. The approach
retains earlier coverage while reducing obsolete object surface in the evaluated
revisits, and the new deployment continues to reconstruct its own changes and
motion. Its gains do not eliminate the cost of memory: separately retained
background meshes can duplicate walls, and incomplete observations can delay
object removal. The results identify conservative object-state reconciliation
as a useful step toward persistent dense mapping, while making geometric fusion,
uncertain association, and longer-horizon validation explicit next challenges.
