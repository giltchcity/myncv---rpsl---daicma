\section{Limitations and Scope}
\label{sec:limitations}

The method is evaluated as a mapping backend with supplied camera poses,
inter-deployment alignment, and persistent instance identities. Automatic
cross-deployment association and robustness to incorrect identities are not
established by these experiments. Imported surface reliability and the fixed
mobility classes are additional assumptions. The sequential absence threshold
has not been calibrated as a global false-removal guarantee; its interaction
with end-of-deployment closure also requires controlled testing.

Partial views can delay removal, as shown by the 3RScan chair and 10.9\%
real-room residue after C. Background geometry is maintained as a mesh rather
than re-fused as uncertain measurements. The recorded real-C analysis attributes
two thirds of carried false surface to duplicate, misaligned geometry. The
system also inherits the motion extractor's minimum-displacement limitation.
Addressing these cases requires changes to inference or reconstruction, not
only a different presentation of the metrics.

The current comparisons demonstrate scene-state transfer and its measured
benefits, but do not establish equivalence to an uninterrupted run or isolate
every proposed component. Complete observed-region results, component ablations,
alignment perturbations, and export/import latency and memory-growth measurements
are not reported. The three selected 3RScan pairs support case-level analysis,
not a benchmark-wide generalization claim.
