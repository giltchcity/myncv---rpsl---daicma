\begin{abstract}
The world does not reset when a mapper does. A robot that returns to a room may find the chair moved and the suitcase
gone. Reloading the old map keeps both at their old sites, and starting over discards every room the robot does not
revisit. Existing updates reason within one visit or carry a map without its reasoning, each for one representation. We
treat visits as interrupted observations of one evolving scene: a change made while the robot is away is decided like one
made while it looks away. One update layer infers when each object state holds, with one likelihood, one prior, and one
decision rule, admits an observation only while its state holds, keeps an earlier map element until current observations
place its surface elsewhere, and passes its state to the next visit. A TSDF, a 3D Gaussian, and an occupancy mapper join
it through four operations. Over three visits to a real room, the TSDF keeps under \prov{7\%} of the surface of departed
objects, against about 60\% for Panoptic Multi-TSDFs. When the second visit to a simulated apartment sees only part of
it, all three maps stay more complete than under their published updates and far more complete than when rebuilt from
scratch.
\end{abstract}
