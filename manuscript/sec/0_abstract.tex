\begin{abstract}
Reconstructing a changing scene requires updating both its geometry and the observations used to build it.
The same earlier image can constrain an unchanged wall while its object or free-space measurements conflict
with the current scene. We present \ours, a unified method for dynamic mapping within and across visits
that connects these updates through scene-state intervals. Change detection uses current observations to
determine which states persist, end, or begin. Map update applies these decisions to associated geometry
and restricts observation contributions to compatible states and regions. New observations reconstruct the
changed scene, while valid earlier information continues to complete it. The same mechanism governs TSDF
fusion, Gaussian optimization, and occupancy integration, with ended states retained for historical queries.
Experiments on four datasets include real and synthetic sequences with both within-visit and cross-visit
changes. All three representations outperform their corresponding baselines in geometry, object
reconstruction, and change detection. Gaussian rendering improves by 0.96--3.43\,dB PSNR over GaME,
and retaining valid geometry on a partial revisit increases recall over rebuilding from scratch by up to
21.1 points.
\end{abstract}
