\begin{abstract}
An observation can be accurate when recorded and still be wrong for the current
map: the object it measured may have moved since then. Repeated mapping therefore
requires deciding which observations remain valid, as well as how to fuse them.
We propose a persistent map-update method based on temporal validity and selective
use of memory. Object-state reasoning identifies changes and excludes observations
of states that have ended. Current valid measurements determine the map where
they provide evidence; previous geometry is retained elsewhere, except where it
conflicts with a currently observed object. This separates the temporal selection
of evidence from the map representation used to integrate it. We give a TSDF
formulation that applies the same selection to object and background geometry
before surface extraction. Our current Khronos-based pipeline is evaluated on
repeated real and synthetic recordings and three public datasets. On real-room
revisits, change F1 is 96.8\% and 100.0\%, and old-location residue is
6.8\% and 10.9\%, compared with 57.1\% and 60.1\% for Panoptic Multi-TSDFs.
On synthetic data, geometry recall is 98.1\%, compared with 78.5\% for the
from-scratch result at similar precision. The central idea is to update a map
from observations that still describe the current world, rather than combine
all observations regardless of when they were valid.
\end{abstract}
