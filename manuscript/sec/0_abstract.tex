\begin{abstract}
Dynamic mapping must update scene geometry and determine how earlier observations can contribute as objects
change during and between visits.
Existing systems handle these updates through rules tied to their own representations, from signed-distance
surfaces to Gaussian primitives and occupancy cells.
We present \ours, a unified method for dynamic mapping within and across visits.
It uses depth observations to infer when each object state exists, then applies these time intervals to both
the map geometry and its observation contributions. Changed geometry leaves the current map, new observations
reconstruct its replacement, and unchanged surfaces continue to accumulate measurements.
Ended states remain available for historical queries, while the saved map and scene state support the next visit.
This mechanism connects to TSDF, Gaussian, and occupancy maps through their native integration procedures.
Across four repeated-visit datasets, all three mappers outperform their native updates in geometry, object
reconstruction, and change detection. On a partial revisit of a simulated apartment, geometry recall improves
over rebuilding from scratch by up to 21.1 points.
\end{abstract}
