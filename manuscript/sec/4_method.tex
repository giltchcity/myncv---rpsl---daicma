\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

A map update must decide which observations still describe the scene and which elements of the earlier map remain.
Both are questions about rays and object states, so our update layer answers them beside an existing mapper
(\cref{fig:method}) with three decisions. \emph{State intervals} record when each object
state holds (\cref{sec:states}). \emph{Valid domains} restrict each observation to the space where the state it
measured still holds (\cref{sec:validity}). The \emph{continuation test} keeps an element of the previous map until
current observations place its surface elsewhere (\cref{sec:memory}). A mapper joins through the \emph{update
interface} and keeps its own integration rule (\cref{sec:representations}). Every element keeps its \emph{lifetime},
so the map of any earlier time remains available, and each run hands its state to the next (\cref{sec:handoff}).

\subsection{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

We address persistent map updating. An existing mapper builds a map from one run through an indoor scene, and later
runs revisit the scene after it has changed. The layer targets a map that is correct at every time: the map at time $t$ shows
the surfaces that held at $t$, and a region that a run leaves unobserved keeps its last observed state. We target
surfaces that disappear, appear, or move, in view, out of view during a run, or between runs. Moving people are masked out of
the static map.

Like Panoptic Multi-TSDFs~\cite{schmid2022panoptic}, the layer starts from posed, segmented RGB-D
frames. An observation
$o$ is the depth ray of one pixel with depth, with measured range $r_o$, time $t_o$, and segment label
$\ell_o$, possibly none. Each physical object $\ell$ has a sequence of geometric states. A state $s$ holds its
reconstructed surface $G_s$ and a state interval $[b_s, d_s)$, and ending a state moves it to history. The map
consists of elements $\mathcal{E}$ that the mapper can keep or end individually: a surface face of a TSDF mesh, a 3D
Gaussian, or an occupied cell. Element $e$ has a position $x_e$, a half-size $h_e$ (half a voxel, three standard
deviations of a Gaussian, or half a cell), an identity $\ell_e$ that is $0$ for the background, and a lifetime
$[c_e, d_e)$. The map at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}(t) = \{\, e \in \mathcal{E} : c_e \le t < d_e \,\}.
  \label{eq:map_at_t}
\end{equation}
Ending an element sets $d_e$ instead of deleting it, so the map of any earlier time remains available.

\subsection{State Intervals: One Likelihood, One Prior, One Decision}
\label{sec:states}

Objects change in three ways: they move in view, change out of view during a run, or change between runs. The layer
decides all three with one likelihood, one prior, and one decision rule, and the three differ only in their evidence
and in the odds they start from. The decisions run in periodic change-detection rounds and yield the interval $[b_s, d_s)$ of every
state. Every state begins at its first observation. A state ended by a successor closes at the round that decides the
handover, or at its last support if that comes later, and one ended without a successor closes midway between its last
evidence and the deciding round.

\paragraph{Evidence.}
The layer reconstructs object states from the segmented depth, masks moving people with the free-space cue of
Dynablox~\cite{schmid2023dynablox} as Khronos does~\cite{schmid2024khronos}, and tests each state against later depth
images. A surface sample of the state is \emph{supported} if the reading at its pixel lies within 5\,cm of its depth,
\emph{seen through} if the reading lies farther, and \emph{occluded} if it lies closer. Only reliable samples vote,
which excludes thin parts, edges, and mislabeled pixels (supplementary material).

\paragraph{Three explanations.}
Let $A$ be the current state of object $\ell$ and $N$ the observations of $\ell$ in the current run that the layer has
not yet associated with $A$. Under $S$, $A$ persists and $N$ shows other parts of it. Under $M$, $A$ has ended and $N$
is its successor. Under $U$, $N$ is spurious: a fragment or a mislabel. Occluded readings, readings outside the view,
and invalid readings have likelihood ratio one, and the remaining readings fall into three disjoint groups.

The first group, the \emph{old-site evidence}, holds the readings that fall on $G_A$ after its last support. Observation $i$ judges $n_i$ reliable
samples and sees through $k_i$ of them, with fraction $f_i = k_i/n_i$. Under $S$, $f$ follows a Beta density $p_A$ that
the layer learns from the object's own in-place observations, and under $M$, $f$ is uniform. A Page
statistic~\cite{page1954cusum} maximizes the evidence over the first observation $j$ after the change:
\begin{equation}
  L_t = \max\Big(0,\ \max_{j \le t} \sum_{i=j}^{t} w_i(j)\,\big[-\log p_A(f_i)\big]\Big), \qquad
  w_i(j) = \min\Big(1,\ \frac{N_i(j)}{N_{\mathrm{rel}}}\Big),
  \label{eq:absence}
\end{equation}
where $N_i(j)$ counts the reliable samples that observation $i$ judges for the first time since observation $j$ and
$N_{\mathrm{rel}}$ counts all reliable samples, so repeated looks at a small patch cannot add up to a whole object. The
second group, the \emph{displacement evidence}, holds the position of $N$ relative to $G_A$: the share of $N$'s surface that lies farther than 10\,cm from
$G_A$ gives an evidence ratio $B_{M:S} \in [0, 2]$ (supplementary material). The third group, the \emph{new-site evidence}, holds the observations
of $N$'s own surface, whose absence likelihood ratios multiply to $L_U$, the likelihood ratio of $U$ against a genuine $N$. Visible motion, flagged by the layer's object
tracker as in Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos}, has likelihood ratio $\infty$ for $M$, so the same rule ends the state at
once.

\paragraph{One prior.}
The prior $q_\ell$ is the probability that a resolved test of $\ell$ ends in a change. It is the posterior
mean of a Beta distribution whose pseudo-counts $\alpha_c, \beta_c$ come from the other objects of $\ell$'s class and whose
counts are $\ell$'s own resolved changes $m_\ell$ and continuations $u_\ell$:
\begin{equation}
  q_\ell = \frac{\alpha_c + m_\ell}{\alpha_c + \beta_c + m_\ell + u_\ell}.
  \label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
We write $q$ for $q_\ell$ below.

\paragraph{One decision rule.}
Every action minimizes the posterior expected loss under two losses. Showing a surface costs the area of the symmetric
difference between the shown and the true current surface, so every display and association decision compares the
posteriors of the explanations. When no successor is observed, wrongly ending a state costs 99 times more than wrongly keeping
it, which puts that boundary at posterior odds of 99. Given the displacement and new-site evidence, and with $v = \frac{1}{2}$ the prior probability that
$N$ is genuine, the three explanations have posteriors
\begin{equation}
  P(S) \propto (1-q)\,v, \qquad P(M) \propto q\,v\,B_{M:S}, \qquad P(U) \propto (1-v)\,L_U.
  \label{eq:smu}
\end{equation}
$A$ ends without a successor when its absence log odds exceed $\ln 99$ and the majority of the same rays sees through
$G_A$. The log odds are $L_t$ for a change during the run and, for an inherited state, $\operatorname{logit} q$ plus the
evidence of the run for a change before it. $N$ replaces $A$ when $P(M) > P(S) + P(U)$, that is,
$q\,B_{M:S} > (1-q) + \frac{1-v}{v}\,L_U$. Merging, display, and the end of a run follow from the same posteriors
(supplementary material), and every posterior threshold comes from the two losses. \Cref{tab:decisions} lists the
evidence of each kind of change.

\subsection{Valid Domains}
\label{sec:validity}

An observation is \emph{temporally valid} only where the state it measured still holds, and it shapes the current
map only there. Let $s$ be the current state of object $\ell$. If $s$ began
in the current run, at $b_s$, the layer removes $\ell$'s labeled pixels with $t_o < b_s$ from the fusion, so an earlier pose of $\ell$ cannot reappear. Every other pixel keeps
its measured surface, but its free space ends a truncation band $T$ before $\lambda_o$, the first intersection of
its ray with a current object, taken from the object's faces or, where they are missing, from the voxels of the object's
own readings. Let $\delta_o(x)$ be the signed distance of $x$ to the measured surface along the ray, positive in front
of it, and $\rho_o(x)$ the range of $x$. The valid domain of observation $o$ at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_o(t) = \{\, x : \delta_o(x) > -T \,\wedge\, \big(\delta_o(x) \le T \,\vee\, \rho_o(x) < \lambda_o - T\big) \,\},
  \label{eq:domain}
\end{equation}
and empty for a removed pixel. For a mapper that optimizes its elements, the valid domain of a keyframe is the set of
elements it may train. The domain depends on $t$ through the state intervals, which change at every
change-detection round, and the layer uses only observations received up to $t$.

\subsection{The Continuation Test}
\label{sec:memory}

The continuation test keeps the map persistent: an element of the previous map continues until observations of the
current run, within their valid domains, place its surface elsewhere. For element $e$ and an observation $o$ whose pixel
sees $x_e$ within its valid domain, let $r = r_o - \rho_o(x_e)$ be the reading minus the range of $x_e$, and let
$\tau_e(\rho) = \max(h_e, \sigma(\rho))$, where $\sigma(\rho)$ is the depth residual of the current reconstruction at
range $\rho$, smoothed across range bins. The observation \emph{supports} $e$ if $|r| \le \tau_e$ and \emph{sees
through} $e$ if $r > \tau_e$, and a reading in front of $e$ casts no direct vote. Let $n_{\mathrm{sup}}$ and
$n_{\mathrm{thr}}$ count the supports and see-throughs over the current run. Direct evidence decides by majority. Without
a majority, $e$ ends only if it lies behind a second layer of the same surface within the truncation band $T$, written
$C(e)$, and otherwise it keeps its prior:
\begin{equation}
  K(e) = \begin{cases}
    [\,n_{\mathrm{sup}} > n_{\mathrm{thr}}\,], & n_{\mathrm{sup}} \ne n_{\mathrm{thr}},\\
    0, & n_{\mathrm{sup}} = n_{\mathrm{thr}},\ C(e),\\
    K_{\mathrm{prior}}(e), & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:continue}
\end{equation}
An element that the previous map shows has $K_{\mathrm{prior}} = 1$, so an unobserved region keeps the earlier map. If
the direct votes are independent and each points the right way with probability $p > \frac{1}{2}$, the log likelihood ratio of removal against
continuation is $(n_{\mathrm{thr}} - n_{\mathrm{sup}})\log\frac{p}{1-p}$, so the majority is the maximum-likelihood
decision for every such $p$. $C(e)$ encodes a property of the representation: a truncation band holds one surface of a
given orientation, so an element behind such a layer duplicates it. Object elements follow their state: the elements of
an ended state leave the current map at once and stay in history.

\subsection{The Update Interface}
\label{sec:representations}

The update interface has four operations: the mapper reports its elements, ends the elements the layer names, accepts
the state intervals, and integrates observations within their valid domains. The mapper supplies the half-size $h_e$
of its elements, its truncation band $T$, and its test $C(e)$, and the layer makes the state decisions and runs the continuation test with the
same code for all three mappers. Each mapper keeps its own integration: the TSDF its weighted average, GaME its photometric
and depth optimization, and wavemap its log-odds integration of occupied space. The supplementary material gives the
details of each backend.

\paragraph{TSDF backend.}
Our TSDF backend is a Khronos pipeline~\cite{schmid2024khronos} that keeps Khronos' active window, tracking, and object
extraction. Khronos' projective integrator fuses each frame within its valid domain into one TSDF with 5\,cm voxels
and truncation $T = 10$\,cm by the weighted running average~\cite{curless1996volumetric,newcombe2011kinectfusion},
\begin{equation}
  F^{+}(x) = \frac{W(x)\,F(x) + w_o(x)\,d_o(x)}{W(x) + w_o(x)},\quad
  W^{+}(x) = \min\big(W(x) + w_o(x),\, W_{\max}\big),\quad x \in \Omega_o(t),
  \label{eq:tsdf}
\end{equation}
with Khronos' distance $d_o$ and weight $w_o$. When a round changes the state intervals, the backend re-integrates the
affected blocks, and Khronos' mesh integrator extracts the present surface. The faces of
the previous map pass \cref{eq:continue}, where $C(e)$ holds if a face lies behind a layer of the present surface within
$T$ and both face the same way. The current map is the present surface with the continuing faces.

\paragraph{Gaussian backend.}
Our Gaussian backend is GaME~\cite{yugay2026game}, which optimizes 3D Gaussians~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians} over
keyframes with photometric and depth losses. Each Gaussian carries a lifetime, and a keyframe at time $t_k$ renders and trains only
$\mathcal{M}(t_k)$, so the observations of an ended state train only the Gaussians of their time. At each keyframe of
the current run, the backend tests every live Gaussian of the previous map at the Gaussian's own pixel, with $\tau_e$ at least 5\,cm. A reading within
$T = 10$\,cm in front of the Gaussian, from the side from which the previous run saw it, \emph{covers} it, and $C(e)$ holds if the run neither supports nor sees through
the Gaussian and covers outnumber the readings farther in front. Earlier Gaussians that the current run supports or
covers are trained by its keyframes, and the others only by the keyframes of their own run. These rules give each keyframe its valid domain, the set of
Gaussians it may train. An earlier Gaussian leaves
the map only by \cref{eq:continue} or with its ended object state.

\paragraph{Occupancy backend.}
Our occupancy backend is wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap}, which accumulates log-odds occupancy in a
wavelet-compressed octree with clamping~\cite{hornung2013octomap}. Its elements are the occupied cells that contain a
measured surface point. Wavemap integrates the occupied space of every frame, and the layer takes over the clearing of
occupied cells: a cell leaves the current map only through \cref{eq:continue} and through object states, and an ended
cell keeps its lifetime for the maps of earlier times. Cells carry no orientation, so $C(e)$ never holds and a tie keeps
the prior.

\subsection{Run Handoff and Implementation}
\label{sec:handoff}

A run exports three things: the map timeline with element lifetimes; the inference state of the objects, with their
current states, intervals, and surfaces, which seeds the next run's decisions; and the shown surface of its final map,
which becomes the next run's memory. It also exports the pooled sensor statistics of the absence test. The next run loads
this state, aligns its frames to the common frame, and runs the same decisions. Every dataset and mapper shares the
constants: the 5\,cm tolerance of the state test, the $60^\circ$ incidence limit, the $\ln 99$ threshold, at least
$\min(30, N_{\mathrm{rel}})$ judged samples per observation, and the 10\,cm state tolerance. The truncation band belongs
to each mapper.
