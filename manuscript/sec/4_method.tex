\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

From posed RGB-D observations, \ours updates a map through changes within and across visits.
A confirmed change updates both the stored geometry and the observations used to reconstruct it.
We connect these operations through scene-state intervals (\cref{fig:method}): \emph{change detection}
determines which states continue, end, or begin (\cref{sec:states}); \emph{map update} applies these decisions
to geometry and observation contributions (\cref{sec:validity}). These decisions drive all three map representations.

\paragraph{Map representation.}
\label{sec:setting}
A state groups observations of an object while its geometry remains unchanged. Additional views complete
that state's surface; after a move, observations at the new location reconstruct a new state.
Object masks (SAM masks in \cref{fig:method}) group surface observations, and associations link these regions
across frames. State $s$ has a surface $G_s$ and a presence interval $[b_s,d_s)$. This interval specifies
both when its geometry belongs in the map and which observations describe it.
Each map element $e$ carries its object association, if available, and a lifetime $[c_e,d_e)$.
Elements are TSDF surface faces, Gaussians, or occupied cells, depending on the representation.
The map at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}(t)=\{e\in\mathcal{E}:c_e\leq t<d_e\}.
  \label{eq:map_at_t}
\end{equation}
Element lifetimes follow scene states and may begin before the elements are allocated.
Closing a lifetime retires an element while preserving its history.

\subsection{Change Detection}
\label{sec:states}

A local observation must establish whether an existing surface has changed or is merely hidden.
Seeing the floor behind an earlier chair provides evidence that the chair has left; seeing another object
in front of it indicates occlusion. We collect these comparisons over the observed surface to decide
which states continue, end, or begin. The same tests apply when a change is discovered after looking
away or returning on another visit.

\paragraph{Surface evidence.}
We project surface samples of $G_s$ into each subsequent depth image.
A sample is supported when depths agree, seen through when the measurement lies farther along the ray,
and occluded when it lies closer. Support and see-through observations provide evidence about the surface;
occlusions, invalid depths, and samples outside the image contribute no absence evidence.
A depth tolerance and reliable surface samples account for measurement noise and reconstruction error.
Object associations assign the evidence to a state.

\paragraph{Change decisions.}
Each patch contributes according to its coverage, even when observed repeatedly.
At round $i$, let $f_i=k_i/n_i$ be the fraction of inspected samples that are seen through.
We model $f_i$ with a Beta density $p_s(f)$ estimated from in-place observations and use a uniform
changed-state model. A Page statistic~\cite{page1954cusum} accumulates their one-sided log-likelihood ratio
$\ell_s(f_i)$ over candidate change times $j$, weighted by newly inspected surface coverage:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  L_t&=\max\!\left(0,\max_{j\leq t}\sum_{i=j}^{t}w_i(j)\,\ell_s(f_i)\right),\\
  w_i(j)&=\frac{\nu_i(j)}{\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:absence}
\end{equation}
Here $\nu_i(j)$ counts reliable samples inspected for the first time since $j$, and $\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}$
counts all reliable samples of the state. Each sample contributes once per candidate window.
We confirm absence when the score exceeds
the decision threshold and see-through evidence outweighs support. Object and class persistence set revisit priors.

Compatible observations extend a state; incompatible observations form candidates. We check each
candidate's geometric relation to the current surface and its support in later observations.
A confirmed successor starts a new state and closes the previous one; unresolved candidates are retained.
Each state's observations reconstruct its geometry.

We detect visible motion using object tracking and free-space cues from Khronos and
Dynablox~\cite{schmid2024khronos,schmid2023dynablox}. Once the object settles, new observations reconstruct
its geometry. Observed motion and changes discovered against the retained map lead to the same updates.

\subsection{Map Update}
\label{sec:validity}

A confirmed change alters both geometry and observation contributions.
Earlier measurements may describe the old object or empty space now occupied by its replacement.
We apply state intervals to both geometry and observations: ended geometry leaves the current map, and
measurements contribute where they remain compatible. Unchanged surfaces accumulate further measurements.

\paragraph{Applying changes to geometry.}
A confirmed change concerns the associated state, including geometry outside the current view.
Its elements therefore close their lifetimes together when the state ends. New observations reconstruct
the successor, while continuing states accumulate geometry. The ended reconstruction remains in
$\mathcal{M}(t)$ at earlier times, so updating the current map also preserves its history.

\paragraph{Selecting observation contributions.}
A depth measurement contributes both a surface and free-space evidence in front of it.
If a chair is placed in front of a previously observed wall, the earlier wall measurement can still
complete the wall, but its free-space contribution must stop before the new chair. We express this
restriction as the observation's \emph{valid domain}. Pixels belonging to ended states are excluded
from the current reconstruction. Other pixels retain their surface measurements, with free-space
updates bounded by newly established object surfaces.
Let observation $o$ have capture time $t_o$ and measured range $r_o$.
For a point $x$ on its ray, define $\delta_o(x)=r_o-\rho_o(x)$, where $\rho_o(x)$ is the range of $x$.
Let $\lambda_o$ be the first intersection with a current object state that began after $t_o$.
With surface band $T$, the spatial domain is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Omega_o(t)=\{x:\;&\delta_o(x)>-T,\\
                  &\delta_o(x)\leq T\ \lor\ \rho_o(x)<\lambda_o-T\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:domain}
\end{equation}
We set $\lambda_o=\infty$ when there is no such intersection and $\Omega_o(t)=\varnothing$ for an excluded pixel.
The surface band preserves measured geometry, while the free-space bound protects new surfaces from
earlier observations. State changes revise the affected domains.

Keyframe optimization follows the same condition. A frame captured at $t_k$ evaluates
its loss on $\mathcal{M}(t_k)$ and updates compatible geometry. An earlier image of the chair
constrains its earlier state, while measurements of the unchanged wall continue to constrain that wall.
Frame selection, trainable geometry, and map-time selection jointly enforce this constraint.
The current map uses only observations received by the current processing time.

\paragraph{Retaining and replacing surfaces.}
\label{sec:memory}
Earlier maps also contain background surfaces and nearby copies displaced by depth or alignment errors.
We retain unobserved regions and replace stale copies of surfaces reconstructed again.
Support and see-through tests within observation domains determine which earlier elements remain.
For an element at $x_e$, we count support and see-through observations within their valid domains, using
$\tau_e=\max(h_e,\sigma(\rho_o(x_e)),\tau_{\min})$ as the depth tolerance.
Here $h_e$ is the element's geometric support scale, $\sigma$ is the calibrated depth-residual scale, and
$\tau_{\min}$ is a representation-specific minimum.
Measurements within $\tau_e$ support the element; measurements farther behind it provide see-through evidence.
Let $n_{\mathrm{sup}}$ and $n_{\mathrm{thr}}$ denote these counts. The retention decision is
\begin{equation}
K(e)=\begin{cases}
  [n_{\mathrm{sup}}>n_{\mathrm{thr}}],&n_{\mathrm{sup}}\neq n_{\mathrm{thr}},\\
  0,&n_{\mathrm{sup}}=n_{\mathrm{thr}},\ C(e),\\
  1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:continue}
\end{equation}
The local replacement condition $C(e)$ holds when a current surface lies just in front of $e$, within its surface band,
with the same orientation. In a tied case, the current estimate replaces this older copy.
The remaining geometry is retained, including unobserved regions. Occlusion alone contributes no evidence for removal.

\paragraph{Continuing across visits.}
\label{sec:handoff}
We save the current map, element lifetimes, object-state summaries, and evidence statistics at the end of a visit.
The next visit resumes from this state in the shared coordinate frame.

\paragraph{Reconstruction in three representations.}
\label{sec:representations}
The same state decisions and observation domains determine the reconstruction in all three representations.
TSDF fusion, Gaussian optimization, and occupancy integration realize these decisions as follows.

\emph{TSDF.} We fuse signed-distance measurements within $\Omega_o(t)$, using the truncation band for $T$.
When a domain changes, we rebuild affected voxel blocks from archived frames in observation order.
The current mesh includes eligible earlier faces selected by \cref{eq:continue}. We use projective
fusion~\cite{curless1996volumetric,newcombe2011kinectfusion} with Khronos' active window, tracking, and
object extraction~\cite{schmid2024khronos}.

\emph{3D Gaussians.} Each keyframe renders $\mathcal{M}(t_k)$ and updates compatible geometry;
ended Gaussians are frozen to preserve their historical reconstruction. Current-visit frames optimize
new and revisited regions, while historical replay maintains earlier geometry outside the current
coverage. These constraints also apply during final refinement. We use GaME's photometric and depth
optimization~\cite{yugay2026game,kerbl2023gaussians} and FlashSplat for mask-to-Gaussian
assignment~\cite{shen2024flashsplat}. Pruned historical Gaussians receive an end time and remain
available to earlier queries.

\emph{Occupancy.} Cells associated with an ended state leave the current map together. Where a newly
measured object surface occupies a cell, the previous visit's free-space contribution is withdrawn
before integration continues. The replacement condition $C(e)$ retires nearby stale copies, while
eligible unobserved cells are retained. Measurements are integrated using wavemap's wavelet
representation and clamped log odds~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap,hornung2013octomap}.
