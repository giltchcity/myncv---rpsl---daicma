\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Our update layer runs beside an existing mapper (\cref{fig:method}). It reads the RGB-D frames, camera poses, and
instance masks that the mapper integrates. From them it decides which object states hold at each time, which
observations may still shape the current map, and which elements of the previous map continue. The mapper then
integrates the selected observations with its own rule. The layer reads the mapper's elements only to test and end
them and keeps all other evidence itself.

\subsection{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

We address persistent map updating. An existing mapper builds a map from one run through an indoor scene, and later
runs revisit the scene after it has changed. The layer keeps the map correct at every time: the map at time $t$ shows
the surfaces that held at $t$, and a region that a run leaves unobserved keeps its last observed state. We target
surfaces that disappear, appear, or move, between two runs or out of view during a run. People and other moving agents
never enter the static map. The mapper keeps its own integration rule.

Run $k$ receives posed RGB-D frames with instance masks. An observation $o$ is the depth ray of one valid pixel, with
camera center $c_o$, measured range $r_o$, time $t_o$, and instance label $\ell_o$, possibly none. No mapper integrates
pixels of dynamic classes such as people. A supplied rigid transform aligns run $k$ with the previous map, and instance
labels refer to the same physical objects across runs. The layer takes the poses, the alignment, and the identities as
given, and leaves the association of objects across runs to the method that supplies the identities. Identities
sharpen its decisions but do not carry them. Without identities, every surface counts as background, and the layer
still ends each surface that later measurements see through and keeps each surface that they leave unobserved.
Identities add the handover of an object that appears at a new site while its old site stays unobserved, and a
history that links the states of one object.

Each physical object $\ell$ has a sequence of geometric states. A state $s$ holds its reconstructed surface $G_s$ and
a validity interval $[b_s, e_s)$. Ending a state moves it from the current map to history. The map consists of
elements $\mathcal{E}$ that the mapper can keep or end individually: a surface face of a TSDF mesh, a 3D Gaussian, or an
occupied cell. Element $e$ has a position $x_e$, a half-size $h_e$ (half a voxel, three standard deviations of a
Gaussian, or half a cell), an identity $\ell_e$ that is $0$ for the background, and a lifetime $[c_e, d_e)$. The map
at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}(t) = \{\, e \in \mathcal{E} : c_e \le t < d_e \,\}.
  \label{eq:map_at_t}
\end{equation}
Ending an element sets $d_e$ instead of deleting the element, so the map of any earlier time remains available.

\subsection{Object States from Projected Evidence}
\label{sec:states}

The layer keeps its own evidence. It stores the frames it reads and detects visible motion with the free-space cue of
Dynablox~\cite{schmid2023dynablox}, as in the active window of Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos}. Moving pixels enter no
integration. The layer also reconstructs object segments from the instance-masked depth of the stored frames, so every
mapper receives the same object states.

To test whether an earlier state persists, we project its surface samples, at most 1{,}500 on a 2.5\,cm grid, into
each later depth image. Let $z$ be the expected depth of a sample and $d$ the depth measured at a pixel. The sample is
supported if $|d - z| \le 5$\,cm at its own pixel or at any pixel of its footprint, and seen through if
$d - z > 5$\,cm at every valid pixel of its footprint. A closer reading occludes the sample, and a pixel without depth
gives no evidence.
% SRC: CODE_MAP_20261010 sec. 8 (on: own pixel within 5 cm or a footprint pixel inside its ball; seen through: every
% footprint pixel valid and beyond). We use only views within $60^\circ$ of the
sample normal.

Thin parts, edges, and mislabeled pixels produce spurious see-through readings, so only reliable samples vote. A
sample is reliable if it was never seen through while its pixel carried the object's label and if it belongs to the
object with non-negative log odds under a two-component mixture. We fit this mixture by expectation maximization to the
counts of identity hits and other observations of all samples. At each update, we score the fraction $f$ of tested
reliable samples that are seen through under a Beta model of $f$ for a present object. The layer learns this model from
the object's confirmed in-place observations and from pooled sensor statistics. A one-sided Page
statistic~\cite{page1954cusum} accumulates the negative log density of $f$. The layer declares the object absent when
the statistic, including the change prior, exceeds $\ln 99$ and seen-through samples outnumber supported ones.

Once a newly reconstructed object has at least 30 reliable surface cells, the layer compares it with the inherited
state. If most of its surface lies within 10\,cm of the inherited surface, it refines that state; otherwise, it starts a
new state and ends the previous one. These decisions produce the intervals $[b_s, e_s)$. A change that happened out of
view receives the interval boundary that the observations before and after it imply.

\subsection{Valid Observation Domains}
\label{sec:validity}

An observation shapes the current map only where the state it measured still holds. For an object whose current state
$s$ began in the current run, the layer excludes its labeled pixels with $t_o < b_s$. If a state of the object ended in
the current run at $e_s$, the layer also excludes the pixels with $t_o < e_s$ that measured its surface, labeled or
not. Every other pixel keeps its measured surface, but its free space ends one truncation distance $T$ before the
first intersection $\lambda_o$ of its ray with the surface of a current object. Let $\delta_o(x)$ be the signed distance
of $x$ to the measured surface along the ray, positive in front of it, and $q_o(x)$ the range of $x$. The valid domain
of observation $o$ at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_o(t) = \{\, x : \delta_o(x) > -T \,\wedge\, \big(\delta_o(x) \le T \,\vee\, q_o(x) < \lambda_o - T\big) \,\},
  \label{eq:domain}
\end{equation}
and empty for an excluded pixel. The domain depends on $t$ through the state intervals, which change at every
change-detection round. An observation therefore enters, leaves, or changes its domain as the state decisions evolve,
and the layer uses only observations received up to $t$.

\subsection{Continuation of the Earlier Map}
\label{sec:memory}

An element of the previous map continues until the valid observations of the current run place its surface
elsewhere. For element $e$ and a valid observation $o$ whose pixel sees $x_e$, let $\delta = \delta_o(x_e)$ and
$\tau_e(q) = \max(h_e, \sigma(q))$, where $\sigma(q)$ is the depth residual of the current reconstruction at range $q$,
estimated per range bin. The observation \emph{hits} $e$ if $|\delta| \le \tau_e$, \emph{sees through} $e$ if
$\delta > \tau_e$, \emph{covers} $e$ if $-T \le \delta < -\tau_e$, and \emph{occludes} $e$ if $\delta < -T$. Here $T$
is the truncation band of the mapper's surface fusion (\cref{sec:representations}). Let $H$, $F$, $C$, and $O$ count
the hits, see-throughs, covers, and occlusions over the current run. The element continues if
\begin{equation}
  K(e) = \begin{cases}
    [\,H \ge F\,], & H + F > 0,\\
    [\,O \ge C\,], & H = F = 0.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:continue}
\end{equation}
Ties and the absence of evidence keep the element, so an unobserved region keeps the earlier map. Suppose each direct
observation points the right way with probability $p > \frac{1}{2}$. Under this symmetric binomial model, the log
likelihood ratio of removal against continuation is $(F - H)\log\frac{p}{1-p}$, and \cref{eq:continue} is the maximum
a posteriori decision under equal priors for every such $p$. Covers and occlusions decide only when direct evidence is
absent. A surface measured within the truncation band in front of $e$ indicates a second layer of the same surface,
whereas a farther surface is an ordinary occluder.

Object elements also follow their state. Elements of an ended state leave the current map and remain in history, and
the new state replaces the elements of an object whose current state began in this run.

\subsection{Connecting a Mapper}
\label{sec:representations}

A mapper joins the layer through four operations: it reports its elements, ends the elements the layer names,
accepts the state intervals, and integrates observations within their valid domains. It keeps its own integration
rule. We connect three mappers, one per representation, and the same code makes the object-state decisions for all
three.

\paragraph{TSDF backend.}
Our TSDF backend is a Khronos pipeline~\cite{schmid2024khronos}. It integrates each frame as it arrives into one TSDF
with the background map's voxel size, 5\,cm, and truncation
$T = 15$\,cm~\cite{curless1996volumetric,newcombe2011kinectfusion}:
% SRC: wt_step12_real/real_a/config.txt and wt_step12_runs/synthetic/ours/session_a/config.txt (voxel 0.05, trunc 0.15).
% The README's table states T = 2v; the runs use 3v.
\begin{equation}
  F^{+}(x) = \frac{W(x)\,F(x) + \min\!\big(1, \delta_o(x)/T\big)}{W(x) + 1},\quad
  W^{+}(x) = W(x) + 1,\quad x \in \Omega_o(t).
  \label{eq:tsdf}
\end{equation}
When a round changes the state intervals, the backend removes every frame whose domain changes and integrates it again
with its new domain. The field therefore always equals \cref{eq:tsdf} applied to all frames so far under the current
states. Marching cubes~\cite{lorensen1987marching} extracts the surface from the cubes whose eight corners are
integrated. Each face takes the identity of the nearest current object geometry within one map voxel of its centroid
and is background otherwise. An object that receives no face keeps its own mesh. The backend tests the faces of the
previous map with \cref{eq:continue} and with two tests that use the present surface. A face is a displaced copy if the
present surface lies on its camera side at a distance $\Delta$ with
$2h < \Delta \le \tau(q) + (|s_{\mathrm{prev}}| + |s_{\mathrm{now}}|)\,q$, where $s$ are the per-run depth scales
estimated from residuals. A face lies inside an object if rays in 64 fixed directions meet the inner side of that
object's present surface more often than its outer side. The current map consists of the present faces and the
continued faces of the previous map.

\paragraph{Gaussian backend.}
Our Gaussian backend is GaME~\cite{yugay2026game}, which optimizes 3D Gaussians~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians} over
keyframes with photometric and depth losses. Each Gaussian carries a lifetime, and a keyframe at time $t_k$ renders and
supervises only $\mathcal{M}(t_k)$, so observations of an ended state train only the Gaussians of their own time. At
every keyframe of the current run, the backend tests the Gaussians of the previous map with \cref{eq:continue}, with
$h_e$ equal to three standard deviations, the 5\,cm tolerance as a lower bound of $\tau_e$, and $T = 10$\,cm, the
truncation of the depth fusion that extracts the surface of the Gaussian map. The current run's keyframes train the
Gaussians that the run observes. The previous run's keyframes alone train the Gaussians that the current run leaves
undecided, so they continue to maintain the memory of their run. We call the Gaussian at which the transmittance
along a ray first drops to $0.5$ the ray's first Gaussian. An optimization step updates only the Gaussians its keyframe
may train, and its loss counts only the pixels whose first Gaussian is one of them. GaME's opacity pruning removes only
Gaussians created in the current run. FlashSplat~\cite{shen2024flashsplat} assigns identities to the Gaussians,
accumulated over keyframes.

GaME's depth loss compares the rendered depth $D = \sum_i w_i z_i$ with the reading $d$, where $w_i$ is the blending
weight of the $i$-th Gaussian along the ray and $z_i$ is its depth. Where the accumulated opacity $A = \sum_i w_i$ is
below one, Gaussians behind the surface can satisfy this loss without contributing color, and other viewpoints then see
them in front of the surface. We separate the two requirements of the depth loss:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{depth}} = A\,\big|D/A - d\big| + \tau_0\,(1 - A),
  \label{eq:depth}
\end{equation}
with $A$ held fixed in the first term and $\tau_0 = 5$\,cm. The first term places the expected depth at the reading,
and the second asks the ray to end there.

\paragraph{Occupancy backend.}
Our occupancy backend is wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap}, which accumulates log-odds occupancy in a
wavelet-compressed octree with clamping~\cite{hornung2013octomap}. Its elements are the occupied cells that contain a
measured surface point. Ending an element sets its cell to the minimum log odds and starts a new generation of the
cell, so a later measurement creates a new element. We set the free-space weight of the measurement model to $10^{-6}$
(the configuration requires a positive value). The cells of the previous map then leave through \cref{eq:continue} and
through object states, not through every ray that passes them.

\subsection{Run Handoff and Implementation}
\label{sec:handoff}

A run exports the map with element lifetimes, the object states with their intervals and surfaces, the pooled sensor
statistics of \cref{sec:states}, and the per-run depth scales of the displaced-copy test. The next run loads this state,
aligns its frames to the common frame, and repeats the same decisions. Every dataset and mapper shares the constants:
the 5\,cm surface tolerance, the $60^\circ$ incidence limit, the $\ln 99$ threshold, 30 reliable cells, and the 10\,cm
refinement distance. The truncation band belongs to each mapper. Evaluation ground truth played no part in choosing
any constant.
