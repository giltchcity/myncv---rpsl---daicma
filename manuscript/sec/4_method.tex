\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We map a scene that changes while it is being mapped. The input is a posed RGB-D stream with SAM
masks~\cite{kirillov2023sam}, which assign each pixel to an object and keep that assignment across frames, and, on a
revisit, the map and the object states of the previous visit. Dynamic mapping raises two questions, and one mechanism
answers both (\cref{fig:method}). The first is how to judge a change: the decision must be reliable although the camera
sees only part of the scene, and it must come as soon as the evidence suffices (\cref{sec:states}). The second is how to
apply it: a change must reach all the geometry it concerns, and it must leave everything else untouched
(\cref{sec:validity}). The mechanism works with object states and the capture times of observations, not with the
primitives of a map, so the same mechanism updates a TSDF, a 3D Gaussian map, and an occupancy map
(\cref{sec:representations}).

\subsection{Object States}
\label{sec:setting}

An observation $o$ is the depth reading of one pixel, with measured range $r_o$, capture time $t_o$, and instance label
$\ell_o$. Each object $\ell$ is a sequence of static states. A state $s$ has a surface $G_s$, reconstructed from the
observations of $\ell$ during the state, and an interval $[b_s, d_s)$ in which it holds. A change ends a state and may
begin another: a moved object begins a new state, reconstructed from the observations at its new place alone. The map
is a set of elements $\mathcal{E}$: the surface faces of a TSDF, the Gaussians of a Gaussian map, or the occupied cells
of an occupancy map. Element $e$ has a position $x_e$, a half-size $h_e$ (half a voxel, three standard deviations of a
Gaussian, or half a cell), an instance label $\ell_e$, which is $0$ for the background, and a lifetime $[c_e, d_e)$. The
elements of a state end with it. The map at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}(t) = \{\, e \in \mathcal{E} : c_e \le t < d_e \,\}.
  \label{eq:map_at_t}
\end{equation}
Ending an element sets $d_e$ instead of deleting it, so the map can be queried at any earlier time.

\subsection{Fast and Reliable Change Decisions}
\label{sec:states}

A camera sees only part of the scene at a time, and most surfaces are out of view or occluded most of the time. A
surface that is not observed is not evidence that it is gone, and a surface seen through at one small patch is not
evidence that the whole object has left. Deciding from each image alone ends surfaces that depth noise or a partial view
only seems to clear, and waiting until the whole object has been seen again delays every decision. We therefore test
existing surfaces directly with the current depth and decide as soon as the readings cover enough of a surface.

\paragraph{Direct evidence.}
We test each state against every later depth image. Each reliable surface sample of $G_s$ is projected into the image.
Reliable samples exclude thin parts, edges, and mislabeled pixels, whose readings pass the surface even while it is in
place. A sample is \emph{supported} if the reading at its pixel lies within a sensor tolerance of its depth, \emph{seen
through} if the reading lies farther, and \emph{occluded} if it lies closer. Only supported and seen-through samples
carry evidence. Occluded samples, samples outside the view, and missing readings carry none, so a decision rests only on
readings that reach the place of the surface.

\paragraph{Accumulated over the surface.}
Even a surface that is still in place is seen through at a small fraction of its samples, because of depth noise and
slight misregistration. If depth image $i$ judges $n_i$ samples of $G_s$ and sees through $k_i$ of them, we model the
fraction $f_i = k_i/n_i$ of a state in place by a Beta density $p_s$. We learn it from the in-place observations of the
object, drawn toward those of all objects while the object has few of its own, so the test adapts to the sensor and to
the object. A change makes $f$ uniform. A Page statistic~\cite{page1954cusum} accumulates the evidence from the first
image $j$ after the change:
\begin{equation}
  L_t = \max\Big(0,\ \max_{j \le t} \sum_{i=j}^{t} w_i(j)\,\big[-\log p_s(f_i)\big]\Big), \qquad
  w_i(j) = \frac{\nu_i(j)}{\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}},
  \label{eq:absence}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_i(j)$ counts the reliable samples that image $i$ judges for the first time since image $j$, and
$\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}$ counts all reliable samples of the state. The weights of a window sum to at most one, so each
sample testifies once. Repeated looks at a small patch therefore cannot add up to the whole object, which keeps the
decision reliable. The state ends as soon as $L_t$ crosses its threshold, without waiting until the whole surface has
been seen again, which keeps it fast. Its interval closes midway between its last support and that decision.

\paragraph{Successors.}
When an object reappears elsewhere, its new observations may show more of the current state, a successor after a move,
or a spurious fragment such as a mislabeled region. Two pieces of evidence separate them: a new surface that lies away
from the current one favors a move, and a new surface that later readings see through is a fragment. The prior
probability $q_\ell$ that object $\ell$ changes, estimated from the resolved decisions of $\ell$ and of the objects of
its class, weighs the two. When a move is more probable than not, the new observations begin the successor state and the
current state ends; until then, they remain a candidate.

\paragraph{In view, out of view, and between visits.}
An object that moves in view is followed by the object tracker, as in Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos}, and its state
ends when the motion starts; the frames after it settles reconstruct its next state. People are detected with the
free-space cue of Dynablox~\cite{schmid2023dynablox} and kept out of the static map. An object that changes while the
camera looks elsewhere, or while the robot is away between two visits, goes through the tests above once a later
observation reaches its place. A revisit thus continues the decisions of the previous visit: an inherited state enters
the same tests, starting from the prior odds $q_\ell$ that its object has changed.

\subsection{Complete Updates without Side Effects}
\label{sec:validity}

Once a state has ended, the map has to change accordingly, and two failures are typical. An update can be incomplete:
removing only the surface that a reading has seen through leaves the rest of the object behind. And it can have side
effects: the frames taken before the change still show the object at its old place and would fuse it back, free space
observed before a new object arrived would erase the new object, earlier keyframes would train a Gaussian map toward the
old scene, and a new estimate of a surface would reinforce a stale copy of it instead of replacing it. Our update
addresses the first failure by acting on the whole state and the second by restricting every observation to the scene
it measured.

\paragraph{Ending the whole state.}
When a state ends, every element of that state ends with it, including parts that no reading has seen through, such as
the back of a moved sofa. The object leaves the current map at once and stays in the maps of earlier times
(\cref{eq:map_at_t}). Its successor, if any, is built from its own observations.

\paragraph{Valid domains.}
An observation is correct for the scene at the time it was taken. A depth image of a chair taken before the chair moved
still describes the floor and the wall around it, but no longer the chair, and an image of empty floor taken before a box
arrived describes free space that the box now occupies. We therefore restrict each observation to its \emph{valid
domain}, the space where the state it measured still holds. A pixel of object $\ell$ taken outside the interval of
$\ell$'s current state, or any pixel of $\ell$ once $\ell$ has no current state, is excluded from the integration of the
current map. Every other pixel keeps its measured surface, but its free space ends a band $T$ before $\lambda_o$, the
range at which its ray first meets the surface of a current object, with $\lambda_o = \infty$ if it meets none. With
$\rho_o(x)$ the range of point $x$ along the ray and $\delta_o(x) = r_o - \rho_o(x)$ its signed distance to the measured
surface, positive in front of it, the valid domain of $o$ at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_o(t) = \{\, x : \delta_o(x) > -T \,\wedge\, \big(\delta_o(x) \le T \,\vee\, \rho_o(x) < \lambda_o - T\big) \,\},
  \label{eq:domain}
\end{equation}
and empty for an excluded pixel. For a map optimized over keyframes, the same rule acts in time: the keyframe taken at
$t_k$ renders and trains only $\mathcal{M}(t_k)$. Old observations thus complete the surfaces that have not changed,
without rebuilding a removed object or erasing a new one. Valid domains change whenever a state interval changes, and
the map at $t$ uses only the observations received up to $t$.

\paragraph{Replacing stale copies.}
\label{sec:memory}
Some earlier geometry has no state that could end: the background, such as a window sash that has been closed, and a
stale copy of a surface that the current visit has measured again, left slightly behind it by small differences in pose
and depth. We test such elements directly. For element $e$ and an observation $o$ whose pixel sees $x_e$ within its
valid domain, let $\tau_e = \max(h_e, \sigma(\rho_o(x_e)), \tau_{\min})$, where $\sigma(\rho)$ is the depth residual of
the current reconstruction at range $\rho$ and $\tau_{\min}$ is a floor, zero unless stated. The observation supports
$e$ if $|\delta_o(x_e)| \le \tau_e$ and sees through $e$ if $\delta_o(x_e) > \tau_e$. A reading in front of $e$ casts no
vote, since the element may be occluded. With $n_{\mathrm{sup}}$ and $n_{\mathrm{thr}}$ the supports and see-throughs
over the current visit, the element is kept with
\begin{equation}
  K(e) = \begin{cases}
    [\,n_{\mathrm{sup}} > n_{\mathrm{thr}}\,], & n_{\mathrm{sup}} \ne n_{\mathrm{thr}},\\
    0, & n_{\mathrm{sup}} = n_{\mathrm{thr}},\ C(e),\\
    1, & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:continue}
\end{equation}
If every vote points the right way with the same probability above one half, the majority is the maximum-likelihood
decision. The condition $C(e)$ holds when a surface that the current visit measured lies in front of $e$ within the band
$T$ and faces the same way. A band holds one surface of a given orientation, so $e$ is then a stale copy, and the new
estimate replaces it instead of reinforcing it. An element that is only occluded or not observed casts no vote against
itself and stays.

\subsection{Three Maps}
\label{sec:representations}

The same mechanism updates three maps. Each map keeps its own integration of measurements; the mechanism decides which
observations it integrates, through the valid domains, which elements it shows, through their lifetimes, and which
earlier elements remain, through \cref{eq:continue}. \Cref{tab:maps} lists the element, the integration, and the valid
domain of each map.

\paragraph{TSDF.}
Our TSDF map builds on Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos} and keeps its active window, tracking, and object extraction.
Khronos' projective integrator fuses each frame within its valid domain by the weighted running
average~\cite{curless1996volumetric,newcombe2011kinectfusion},
\begin{equation}
  F^{+}(x) = \frac{W(x)\,F(x) + \omega_o(x)\,\phi_o(x)}{W(x) + \omega_o(x)},\quad
  W^{+}(x) = \min\big(W(x) + \omega_o(x),\, W_{\max}\big),\quad x \in \Omega_o(t),
  \label{eq:tsdf}
\end{equation}
with Khronos' signed distance $\phi_o$ and weight $\omega_o$, and $T$ is its truncation band. When a decision changes a
state interval, the blocks whose valid domains changed are integrated again from the stored frames in their order. The
current map is the surface extracted from this volume together with the faces of the earlier map that
\cref{eq:continue} keeps.

\paragraph{3D Gaussians.}
Our Gaussian map builds on GaME~\cite{yugay2026game}, which optimizes 3D Gaussians~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians} over
keyframes with photometric and depth losses, and FlashSplat~\cite{shen2024flashsplat} assigns the instance labels to the
Gaussians. Each Gaussian carries a lifetime, and the keyframe taken at $t_k$ renders and trains only
$\mathcal{M}(t_k)$. Within it, a keyframe of the current visit trains the Gaussians that the visit created, supports, or
covers, and an earlier keyframe trains those that the current visit has not covered. Keyframes of the current visit test
each Gaussian of the earlier map at the Gaussian's own pixel by \cref{eq:continue}. A Gaussian that GaME's opacity
pruning removes is ended instead of deleted, and the refinement after the last visit trains each Gaussian only with the
keyframes whose valid domain contains it.

\paragraph{Occupancy.}
Our occupancy map builds on wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap}, which accumulates log-odds occupancy in a
wavelet-compressed octree with clamping~\cite{hornung2013octomap} and lowers the occupancy of the cells that a reading
passes through. Its elements are the occupied cells that contain a measured surface point, and the cells of an ended
state end together, whether or not a ray reaches them again. Where the current visit first measures an object surface
in a cell that an earlier visit observed as free, the free evidence of the earlier visit is withdrawn from that cell,
which bounds the old free space as \cref{eq:domain} does, so the new surface does not have to overcome it. An earlier
cell for which $C(e)$ holds ends as a stale copy.

\subsection{Across Visits}
\label{sec:handoff}

At the end of a visit, we save the map with the lifetimes of its elements, the states of all objects with their
intervals and surfaces, and the in-place statistics of the absence test. The next visit loads them, aligns its frames to
the common frame, and continues the same decisions from where the previous visit stopped.
