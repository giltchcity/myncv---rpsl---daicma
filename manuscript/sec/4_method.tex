\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\ours maintains a map of a changing scene from posed RGB-D observations, continuing from the saved map when the robot
returns. As shown in \cref{fig:method}, change detection compares new observations with existing surfaces to identify
changes (\cref{sec:states}). Map update uses these decisions to retire outdated geometry and select the observations
used for reconstruction (\cref{sec:validity}). New content is reconstructed from current observations, while unchanged
regions continue to accumulate measurements. We implement this shared mechanism through the native updates of TSDF,
Gaussian, and occupancy maps.

\paragraph{Map representation.}
\label{sec:setting}
An object state $s$ consists of a reconstructed surface $G_s$ and an interval $[b_s,d_s)$ during which it is present.
Observations of an unchanged object extend its surface; after a move, the new location is reconstructed from its own
observations. Object masks and their associations are supplied by the mapping front end.
Each map element $e$ carries its object association, if available, and a lifetime $[c_e,d_e)$.
Elements are TSDF surface faces, Gaussians, or occupied cells, depending on the representation.
The map at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}(t)=\{e\in\mathcal{E}:c_e\leq t<d_e\}.
  \label{eq:map_at_t}
\end{equation}
An element's lifetime follows the represented surface and can therefore begin before the element was allocated by the
mapper. Closing the lifetime removes it from the current map while preserving it for historical queries.

\subsection{Change Detection}
\label{sec:states}

Depth ordering distinguishes a surface that has disappeared from one hidden by an occluder.
We use this relation to test existing surfaces and combine the evidence over the observed portion of each object.
The resulting decisions specify which states persist, end, or begin, together with their time intervals.

\paragraph{Surface evidence.}
We project surface samples of $G_s$ into each subsequent depth image.
A sample is supported when the measured depth agrees with its predicted depth, seen through when the measurement lies
farther along the ray, and occluded when it lies closer. Supported and seen-through samples provide evidence about
the surface; occluded samples, invalid depths, and samples outside the image contribute no absence evidence.
The comparison uses a depth tolerance and reliable surface samples to account for measurement noise and reconstruction
error. Object associations determine which state receives the evidence.

\paragraph{Change decisions.}
We combine the evidence using a coverage-weighted sequential test. At observation round $i$, let $f_i=k_i/n_i$ be the
fraction of the $n_i$ inspected samples that are seen through. A Beta density $p_s(f)$, estimated from in-place
observations, models this fraction while the state is present; the changed-state model is uniform.
A Page statistic~\cite{page1954cusum} tests candidate
change times $j$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  L_t&=\max\!\left(0,\max_{j\leq t}\sum_{i=j}^{t}w_i(j)\,\ell_s(f_i)\right),\\
  w_i(j)&=\frac{\nu_i(j)}{\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:absence}
\end{equation}
Here $\ell_s(f_i)$ is the one-sided log-likelihood ratio against the in-place model. The count $\nu_i(j)$ includes
reliable samples inspected at round $i$ for the first time since candidate time $j$; $\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}$ counts all
reliable samples of the state.
Each sample contributes once per candidate window, so repeated views of one patch retain their actual spatial coverage.
We confirm absence when the score exceeds the decision threshold and see-through evidence outweighs support.
Object and class persistence statistics supply prior odds for changes preceding a revisit.

New observations at another location also provide evidence of relocation. We check their geometric relation to the
current surface and their support in subsequent observations before committing a successor. Compatible observations
extend a continuing state; a confirmed successor starts a new state and closes the previous one.
Unresolved observations remain candidates. These decisions determine the state intervals used by map update.

Visible motion is supplied by the mapping front end, including the tracking and free-space motion cues of
Khronos and Dynablox~\cite{schmid2024khronos,schmid2023dynablox}. Geometry is reconstructed from observations of the
object after it settles. Changes discovered after looking away or returning to the scene are assessed against the
retained map using the surface tests above. Both sources of change feed the same map-update mechanism.

\subsection{Map Update}
\label{sec:validity}

The detected changes determine both which geometry remains in the map and which observations can update it.
An earlier image of a chair and a later image of the exposed floor describe different states of the same location.
Likewise, a newly placed object occupies space that earlier depth images recorded as empty.
We use the inferred intervals to assign observations to the geometry present at their capture times and to revise
their contributions to the current map.

\paragraph{Applying changes to geometry.}
When a state ends, its associated elements close their lifetimes together, including parts outside the current view.
The successor is built from its own observations, while unchanged states continue to accumulate geometry.
This applies a confirmed object change to its full reconstruction and retains its previous geometry in
$\mathcal{M}(t)$ for earlier times.

\paragraph{Selecting observation contributions.}
An observation's valid domain specifies where its contribution is compatible with the reconstructed state.
For volumetric fusion, pixels belonging to an earlier state of a changed object are excluded from the current
reconstruction. Other pixels retain their measured surface contribution, while their free-space contribution is
bounded by surfaces of current objects.
Let observation $o$ have capture time $t_o$ and measured range $r_o$.
For a point $x$ on its ray, define $\delta_o(x)=r_o-\rho_o(x)$, where $\rho_o(x)$ is the range of $x$.
Let $\lambda_o$ be the first intersection with a current object state that began after $t_o$.
With surface band $T$, the spatial domain is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Omega_o(t)=\{x:\;&\delta_o(x)>-T,\\
                  &\delta_o(x)\leq T\ \lor\ \rho_o(x)<\lambda_o-T\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:domain}
\end{equation}
We set $\lambda_o=\infty$ when there is no such intersection and $\Omega_o(t)=\varnothing$ for an excluded pixel.
The surface band preserves measured geometry, while the free-space bound protects newly established surfaces from
earlier empty-space observations. A state change revises the domains of affected observations.

For keyframe optimization, the same temporal condition selects the map rendered for each frame:
a keyframe captured at $t_k$ evaluates its loss on $\mathcal{M}(t_k)$.
Thus a historical image of the chair is evaluated against its earlier state, while observations of the unchanged wall
continue to constrain that wall. The current map uses only observations received up to the current processing time.

\paragraph{Retaining and replacing surfaces.}
\label{sec:memory}
Earlier geometry that remains eligible for the current map is checked against the current visit.
This includes background surfaces and local copies displaced by small depth or alignment errors.
For an element at $x_e$, we count support and see-through observations within their valid domains, using
$\tau_e=\max(h_e,\sigma(\rho_o(x_e)),\tau_{\min})$ as the depth tolerance.
Here $h_e$ is the element's geometric support scale, $\sigma$ is the calibrated depth-residual scale, and
$\tau_{\min}$ is a representation-specific minimum.
Measurements within $\tau_e$ support the element; measurements farther behind it provide see-through evidence.
Let $n_{\mathrm{sup}}$ and $n_{\mathrm{thr}}$ denote these counts. The retention decision is
\begin{equation}
K(e)=\begin{cases}
  [n_{\mathrm{sup}}>n_{\mathrm{thr}}],&n_{\mathrm{sup}}\neq n_{\mathrm{thr}},\\
  0,&n_{\mathrm{sup}}=n_{\mathrm{thr}},\ C(e),\\
  1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:continue}
\end{equation}
The local replacement condition $C(e)$ holds when a current surface lies just in front of $e$, within its surface band,
with the same orientation. In a tied case, this condition lets the current estimate replace the older copy.
Otherwise, the previous geometry is retained, including areas with no new observations.
Occlusion alone contributes no evidence for removal.

\paragraph{Backend integration.}
\label{sec:representations}
Each mapper provides four operations: report its elements, end selected elements, accept state intervals, and integrate
observations within their valid domains. The following implementations retain the native geometry update of each mapper.

\emph{TSDF.} We retain Khronos' active window, tracking, and object extraction~\cite{schmid2024khronos}.
Its projective integrator averages signed-distance measurements~\cite{curless1996volumetric,newcombe2011kinectfusion}
within $\Omega_o(t)$, with $T$ set to its truncation band.
When a domain changes, affected voxel blocks are rebuilt from archived frames in observation order.
The current surface combines the resulting TSDF mesh with eligible earlier faces retained by \cref{eq:continue}.

\emph{3D Gaussians.} We use GaME's photometric and depth optimization~\cite{yugay2026game,kerbl2023gaussians}, with
FlashSplat for mask-to-Gaussian assignment~\cite{shen2024flashsplat}.
Each keyframe renders $\mathcal{M}(t_k)$, and ended Gaussians are frozen to preserve their historical geometry.
Current-visit frames optimize new and revisited regions; historical replay maintains earlier geometry outside the
current coverage. The same temporal selection is used during final refinement.
Pruned historical Gaussians receive an end time and remain available to earlier queries.

\emph{Occupancy.} We retain wavemap's wavelet representation and clamped log-odds
integration~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap,hornung2013octomap}.
Cells associated with an ended state leave the current map together. Where a newly measured object surface occupies
a cell, the previous visit's free-space contribution is withdrawn before continuing integration.
The replacement condition $C(e)$ retires nearby stale copies, while eligible unobserved cells are retained.

\paragraph{Continuing across visits.}
\label{sec:handoff}
We save the current map, element lifetimes, object-state summaries, and evidence statistics at the end of a visit.
The next visit expresses its observations in the shared coordinate frame and resumes the same detection and update
procedures from this retained state.
