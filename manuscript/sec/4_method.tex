\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Our goal is a map that stays correct while the scene changes. The input is a posed RGB-D stream with SAM
masks~\cite{kirillov2023sam}, which assign each pixel to an object and keep that assignment across frames. When an
object moves, the map should drop it from its old place and rebuild it where it now stands, while static regions keep
accumulating observations and regions out of view keep their last observed geometry. Our central idea is to decide
changes in time rather than in the primitives of a map: every change is the end or the beginning of an object state, and
every map element and every observation holds over a time interval. One mechanism then serves every kind of change and
every kind of map. It has two parts (\cref{fig:method}). Judging change decides from the current depth when object
states end and begin (\cref{sec:states}). Applying change uses these intervals to end the geometry of past states, to
restrict every observation to the scene it measured, and to keep the earlier map wherever no observation contradicts it
(\cref{sec:validity}). We apply it to a TSDF, a 3D Gaussian map, and an occupancy map, each of which keeps its own way of
integrating a measurement (\cref{sec:representations}).

\subsection{States and Lifetimes}
\label{sec:setting}

An observation $o$ is the depth reading of one pixel, with measured range $r_o$, capture time $t_o$, and instance label
$\ell_o$. Each object $\ell$ is a sequence of static states. A state $s$ has a surface $G_s$, reconstructed from the
observations of $\ell$ during the state, and an interval $[b_s, d_s)$ in which it holds. A moved object begins a new
state, reconstructed from the observations at its new place alone. The map is a set of elements $\mathcal{E}$: the
surface faces of a TSDF, the Gaussians of a Gaussian map, or the occupied cells of an occupancy map. Element $e$ has a
position $x_e$, a half-size $h_e$ (half a voxel, three standard deviations of a Gaussian, or half a cell), an instance
label $\ell_e$, which is $0$ for the background, and a lifetime $[c_e, d_e)$. The elements of a state end with it. The
map at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}(t) = \{\, e \in \mathcal{E} : c_e \le t < d_e \,\}.
  \label{eq:map_at_t}
\end{equation}
Ending an element sets $d_e$ instead of deleting it, so the map of every earlier time remains available.

In these terms, every kind of change ends one state and may begin another, and the kinds differ only in the evidence
they leave. An object moved in view ends its state while the camera watches the motion. An object changed while the
camera looks elsewhere, or while the robot is away between two visits, ends its state when a later observation reaches
its place again, which is why a visit inherits the states of the previous visit and continues them. The maps differ only
in their elements and in how they integrate a measurement. How the end of a state acts on the map is therefore the same
for every kind of change and every map.

\subsection{Judging Change}
\label{sec:states}

A camera sees only part of the scene at a time, and most surfaces are out of view or occluded most of the time. A
surface that is not observed is not evidence that it is gone, and a surface seen through at one small patch is not
evidence that the whole object has left. Deciding from each image alone ends surfaces that depth noise or a partial view
only seems to clear, and waiting until the whole object has been seen again delays every decision. We therefore judge a
state only from readings that reach its surface, and we count them by how much of the surface they cover.

\paragraph{Evidence from the current depth.}
We test each state against every later depth image. Each reliable surface sample of $G_s$ is projected into the image.
Reliable samples exclude thin parts, edges, and mislabeled pixels, whose readings pass the surface even while it is in
place. A sample is \emph{supported} if the reading at its pixel lies within a sensor tolerance of its depth, \emph{seen
through} if the reading lies farther, and \emph{occluded} if it lies closer. Only supported and seen-through samples
carry evidence. Occluded samples, samples outside the view, and missing readings carry none, so a surface that the
camera does not see stays as it is.

\paragraph{From samples to the whole object.}
Even a surface that is still in place is seen through at a small fraction of its samples, because of depth noise and
slight misregistration. If depth image $i$ judges $n_i$ samples of $G_s$ and sees through $k_i$ of them, we model the
fraction $f_i = k_i/n_i$ of a state in place by a Beta density $p_s$. We learn it from the in-place observations of the
object, drawn toward those of all objects while the object has few of its own, so the test adapts to the sensor and to
the object. A change makes $f$ uniform. A Page statistic~\cite{page1954cusum} accumulates the evidence from the first
image $j$ after the change:
\begin{equation}
  L_t = \max\Big(0,\ \max_{j \le t} \sum_{i=j}^{t} w_i(j)\,\big[-\log p_s(f_i)\big]\Big), \qquad
  w_i(j) = \frac{\nu_i(j)}{\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}},
  \label{eq:absence}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_i(j)$ counts the reliable samples that image $i$ judges for the first time since image $j$, and
$\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}$ counts all reliable samples of the state. The weights of a window sum to at most one, so each
sample testifies once. Repeated looks at a small patch therefore cannot add up to the whole object, and the state ends
as soon as $L_t$ crosses its threshold, without waiting until the whole surface has been seen again. Its interval closes
midway between its last support and that decision.

\paragraph{Successors.}
A state can also end because its object appears elsewhere. New observations of the object at another place may show
more of the current state, a successor after a move, or a spurious fragment such as a mislabeled region. Three pieces of
evidence separate them. A new surface that lies away from the current one favors a move, and a new surface that later
readings see through is a fragment. The prior probability $q_\ell$ that object $\ell$ changes, estimated from the
resolved decisions of $\ell$ and of the objects of its class, weighs the two.
When a move is more probable than not, the new observations begin the successor state and the current state ends; until
then, they remain a candidate. A state inherited from the previous visit enters the same tests, starting from the prior
odds $q_\ell$ that its object has changed.

\paragraph{Motion in view.}
An object that moves in view is followed by the object tracker, as in Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos}. Its state ends
when the motion starts, and the frames after it settles reconstruct its next state. People are detected with the
free-space cue of Dynablox~\cite{schmid2023dynablox} and kept out of the static map.

\subsection{Applying Change}
\label{sec:validity}

A decision alone does not update a map. After a chair moves, the frames taken before still show it at its old place,
the free space observed before a box arrived would carve into the box, and earlier keyframes would train a Gaussian map
toward the old scene. Discarding the earlier observations or the earlier map would remove these conflicts, but also
everything they still describe correctly. Applying a change therefore means acting on exactly the geometry and the
observations that the change concerns.

\paragraph{Ending a state ends its geometry.}
When a state ends, every element of that state ends with it, including parts that no reading has seen through, such as
the back of a moved sofa. The object leaves the current map and remains in the maps of earlier times
(\cref{eq:map_at_t}). The observations that saw it remain as well, and the integration of the map would rebuild the
object from them.

\paragraph{Valid domains.}
An observation is correct for the scene at the time it was taken. A depth image of a chair taken before the chair moved
still describes the floor and the wall around it, but no longer the chair, and an image of empty floor taken before a box
arrived describes free space that the box now occupies. We therefore restrict each observation to its \emph{valid
domain}, the space where the state it measured still holds. A pixel of object $\ell$ taken outside the interval of
$\ell$'s current state, or any pixel of $\ell$ once $\ell$ has no current state, is excluded from the integration of the
current map. Every other pixel keeps its measured surface, but its free space ends a band $T$ before $\lambda_o$, the
range at which its ray first meets the surface of a current object, with $\lambda_o = \infty$ if it meets none. With
$\rho_o(x)$ the range of point $x$ along the ray and $\delta_o(x) = r_o - \rho_o(x)$ its signed distance to the measured
surface, positive in front of it, the valid domain of $o$ at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_o(t) = \{\, x : \delta_o(x) > -T \,\wedge\, \big(\delta_o(x) \le T \,\vee\, \rho_o(x) < \lambda_o - T\big) \,\},
  \label{eq:domain}
\end{equation}
and empty for an excluded pixel. For a map optimized over keyframes, the same rule acts in time: the keyframe taken at
$t_k$ renders and trains only $\mathcal{M}(t_k)$. GaME instead masks the regions of earlier keyframes that conflict with
the current map~\cite{yugay2026game}. With valid domains, an earlier keyframe keeps training the scene it saw, both the
past state and the unchanged surroundings, without pulling the current map back to it. Valid domains change whenever a
state interval changes, and the map at $t$ uses only the observations received up to $t$.

\paragraph{Keeping the earlier map.}
\label{sec:memory}
Valid domains follow object states, and two kinds of earlier geometry have no state that could end. One is the
background, such as a window sash that has been closed since the previous visit. The other is a stale copy of a surface
that the current visit has measured again, left slightly behind it by small differences in pose and depth between the
visits. We therefore
test each element of the earlier map directly. For element $e$ and an observation $o$ whose pixel sees $x_e$ within its
valid domain, let $\tau_e = \max(h_e, \sigma(\rho_o(x_e)), \tau_{\min})$, where $\sigma(\rho)$ is the depth residual of
the current reconstruction at range $\rho$ and $\tau_{\min}$ is a floor, zero unless stated. The observation supports
$e$ if $|\delta_o(x_e)| \le \tau_e$ and sees through $e$ if $\delta_o(x_e) > \tau_e$. A reading in front of $e$ casts no
vote, since the element may be occluded. With $n_{\mathrm{sup}}$ and $n_{\mathrm{thr}}$ the supports and see-throughs over
the current visit, the element is kept with
\begin{equation}
  K(e) = \begin{cases}
    [\,n_{\mathrm{sup}} > n_{\mathrm{thr}}\,], & n_{\mathrm{sup}} \ne n_{\mathrm{thr}},\\
    0, & n_{\mathrm{sup}} = n_{\mathrm{thr}},\ C(e),\\
    1, & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:continue}
\end{equation}
If every vote points the right way with the same probability above one half, the majority is the maximum-likelihood
decision. The condition $C(e)$ holds when a surface that the current visit measured lies in front of $e$ within the band
$T$ and faces the same way. A band holds one surface of a given orientation, so $e$ is then a stale copy and makes way
for the new estimate. Every other element remains, so a region that the current visit does not observe keeps the
earlier map.

\subsection{Three Maps}
\label{sec:representations}

We apply the mechanism to three maps that share nothing but the scene. Each keeps its own integration of measurements,
and the mechanism changes three things in it: which observations it integrates, through the valid domains, which
elements it shows, through their lifetimes, and which earlier elements remain, through \cref{eq:continue}.

\paragraph{TSDF.}
Our TSDF map builds on Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos} and keeps its active window, tracking, and object extraction.
Khronos' projective integrator fuses each frame within its valid domain by the weighted running
average~\cite{curless1996volumetric,newcombe2011kinectfusion},
\begin{equation}
  F^{+}(x) = \frac{W(x)\,F(x) + \omega_o(x)\,\phi_o(x)}{W(x) + \omega_o(x)},\quad
  W^{+}(x) = \min\big(W(x) + \omega_o(x),\, W_{\max}\big),\quad x \in \Omega_o(t),
  \label{eq:tsdf}
\end{equation}
with Khronos' signed distance $\phi_o$ and weight $\omega_o$, and $T$ is its truncation band. When a decision changes a
state interval, the blocks whose valid domains changed are integrated again from the stored frames in their order. The
current map is the surface extracted from this volume together with the faces of the earlier map that
\cref{eq:continue} keeps.

\paragraph{3D Gaussians.}
Our Gaussian map builds on GaME~\cite{yugay2026game}, which optimizes 3D Gaussians~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians} over
keyframes with photometric and depth losses, and FlashSplat~\cite{shen2024flashsplat} assigns the instance labels to the
Gaussians. Each Gaussian carries a lifetime, and the keyframe taken at $t_k$ renders and trains only
$\mathcal{M}(t_k)$. Within it, a keyframe of the current visit trains the Gaussians that the visit created, supports, or
covers, and an earlier keyframe trains those that the current visit has not covered. Keyframes of the current visit test
each Gaussian of the earlier map at the Gaussian's own pixel by \cref{eq:continue}. A Gaussian that GaME's opacity
pruning removes is ended instead of deleted, and the refinement after the last visit trains each Gaussian only with the
keyframes whose valid domain contains it.

\paragraph{Occupancy.}
Our occupancy map builds on wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap}, which accumulates log-odds occupancy in a
wavelet-compressed octree with clamping~\cite{hornung2013octomap}. Its own update lowers the occupancy of the cells a
reading passes through, so a cell that the current visit sees through leaves the map by wavemap's integration alone. Its
elements are the occupied cells that contain a measured surface point, and the cells of an ended state end together.
Where the current visit first measures an object surface in a cell that an earlier visit observed as free, the free
evidence of the earlier visit is withdrawn from that cell, which bounds the old free space as \cref{eq:domain} does, so
the new surface does not have to overcome it. An earlier cell for which $C(e)$ holds ends as a stale copy.

\subsection{Across Visits}
\label{sec:handoff}

At the end of a visit, we save the map with the lifetimes of its elements, the states of all objects with their
intervals and surfaces, and the in-place statistics of the absence test. The next visit loads them, aligns its frames to
the common frame, and continues the same decisions from where the previous visit stopped.
