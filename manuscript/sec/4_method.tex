\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem and Notation}
\label{sec:problem}

A deployment $k$ observes an RGB-D stream $Z^k$ with poses and instance masks
carrying \emph{physical identities} (one id per physical object, stable across
deployments). Session $k$ starts from the persistent state $\mathcal{S}^{k-1}$
exported by the previous deployment and must (i) reconcile the changes that
happened while the robot was away (D3), (ii) track motion it observes (D1),
(iii) reconcile changes that happen while it looks elsewhere (D2), and (iv)
export $\mathcal{S}^k=\Phi(\mathcal{S}^{k-1},Z^k)$ for the next deployment. The
map is queried at a time $t$ and must answer what the world was like at $t$,
not return a union of everything ever observed.

The persistent state holds, for every identity $o$, an ordered list of
\emph{temporal fragments} $F_o=\{f_1,\dots\}$; a fragment is one geometric
state of the object: a private mesh $G_f$, its bounding box, birth and (if
ended) death time, and provenance (dynamic trajectory, reconstruction frames,
session). Exactly one fragment per identity is \emph{current}. The state also
holds the current background mesh, the 4D history (one full scene snapshot per
reconciliation round) and the sensor statistics learned while the object states
were observed (\cref{sec:absence}). All decisions below are decisions about
fragments; the D1/D2/D3 events reported by the system are read off the fragment
lists (a fragment that ends is a \emph{disappeared} event, a fragment that is
born elsewhere an \emph{appeared} event, a fragment with a trajectory an
observed motion).

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

We build on Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos}: its active window integrates
RGB-D into a TSDF, detects and tracks moving clusters, and extracts object
segments once they leave the window; its backend keeps the dense scene graph and
verifies stored surfaces against later sensor rays. Everything that concerns
identity, temporal state and persistence is ours: (1) a per-identity fragment
registry with the ingestion, closing and hand-off rules of
\cref{sec:states}; (2) projected physical evidence and the observed-absence test
that decides when a stored surface has been seen empty (\cref{sec:absence});
(3) the same-state test that decides whether a re-observed identity is the state
that was inherited (\cref{sec:samestate}); (4) the session contract that exports
and re-imports scene state (\cref{sec:contract}). No rule branches on the
dataset; the same binary runs on synthetic, real, and public data.

\subsection{Identity and Fragment States}
\label{sec:states}

\paragraph{Ingestion.}
A segment $s$ extracted by the active window carries the identity $o$, its mesh,
its observation interval $[t_0,t_1]$ and whether the tracker saw it move. If it
moved, the current fragment of $o$ ends at $t_0$ and $s$ starts a new one with
the trajectory attached (D1). Otherwise $s$ is compared with the current fragment
$c$ of $o$. If $c$ was inherited from a previous session, $s$ is kept apart as
the \emph{session copy} of $o$ until measured evidence relates the two
(\cref{sec:samestate}). If $c$ was built in this session and $s$ shares space
with it (some vertex of $s$ falls into an occupied $5$\,cm cell of $c$), $s$
refines $c$ in place---\emph{unless} $c$ was observed empty while $s$ was being
observed: every reliable sample of $c$ that was judged in that interval was seen
through and no ray met $c$'s identity. Shared space is where an object moved by
less than its own size lands; it is not proof that the old state still holds.
An $s$ that shares no space, or whose $c$ was observed empty, becomes the
\emph{candidate} of $o$: a second hypothesis that is neither merged nor deleted
until the evidence of \cref{sec:absence} decides.

\paragraph{Closing and hand-off.}
Each reconciliation round (every fifth backend update, $\approx 2$\,s of sensor
time) measures the current fragment and the candidate with the evidence of
\cref{sec:absence}. The current fragment ends when it is observed absent, or when
the candidate lies at a different site and the current fragment has no support
at all (a moved object seen at its new site before its old site is looked at,
D2). A candidate within the extent of the current fragment cannot end it alone.
A candidate is absorbed into the current fragment only in a round in which the
current fragment has support; absorbing presupposes presence. When the session
ends, fragments whose absence is pending are closed, and for static identities
the inherited fragment and the session copy are united.

\subsection{Projected Physical Evidence and Observed Absence}
\label{sec:absence}

\paragraph{Per-sample verdicts.}
The surface of a fragment is sampled into $5$\,cm cells. For every frame whose
frustum contains a cell within the sensor range and at incidence $\le 60^\circ$,
the cell receives one verdict from the depth image and the instance mask at its
projection: \emph{on surface} (depth within the sensor tolerance $\tau_s=5$\,cm
of the cell), \emph{identity} (on surface and the mask carries $o$),
\emph{foreign} (on surface under another label), \emph{seen through} (measured
depth more than $\tau_s$ behind the cell), or no verdict (occluded, out of
range). Seen-through verdicts are the only evidence of absence; a foreign label
is a missed detection or another surface and decides nothing.

\paragraph{Reliable samples.}
Not every stored cell is a real surface: silhouettes of depth slivers, specular
drop-outs and grazing views produce cells that are seen through while the object
plainly stands there (on our real sensor, $12$--$55\%$ of the cells of static
objects). A cell is \emph{reliable} once the object's own identity has been
measured on it in at least three frames and it has never been seen through
while the object was identified in place; inherited surfaces are reliable by
construction. Only reliable cells vote.

\paragraph{Look-wise test.}
One \emph{look} is one reconciliation round. For a look with $n\ge 1$ judged
reliable cells, $f$ is the share seen through. Under ``the object stands here'',
$f$ follows a Beta law fitted to the object's own history of looks in which it
was identified in place (its own missed-detection behaviour), shrunk towards the
population of objects measured under the same sensor (three pseudo-looks; the
spread never narrower than the between-object spread); objects without history
use the population prior. Under ``the object is gone'' $f$ is uniform. The
one-sided log-likelihood ratio of the look (only \emph{more} seen-through than
usual speaks for absence) is weighted by the share of the reliable surface judged
for the first time in the current accumulation and accumulated by a CUSUM; the
fragment is observed absent when the accumulation exceeds $\ln 99$ (a $1\%$
error bound, the only decision constant of the system), provided the rays that
met the surface are outnumbered by rays that passed through it. Looks in which
the object is identified in place update the object's Beta history; the pooled
population statistics are exported with the map and loaded by the next session
(\cref{sec:contract}). A look with no judged cell is neither scored nor learned
from.

\subsection{Same-State Test for Inherited Fragments}
\label{sec:samestate}

A movable identity (a class that can be moved by a person) whose current
fragment was inherited may be reconstructed again by the session. Once that
session copy is \emph{established}---at least $30$ reliable cells, the sample
count one look needs---the inherited fragment is the same state only if the
majority of the copy lies within $\tau_o=10$\,cm of the inherited surface;
$\tau_o$ is the tolerance with which the evaluation protocol itself defines a
state as represented, i.e.\ the granularity at which a state is a state, whereas
$\tau_s$ is the sensor's. If the copy lies mostly elsewhere, one identity cannot
be in two places: the inherited fragment ends and the copy becomes current,
whatever the old site shows so far (a table turned on its spot ends at
$18.7$\,s instead of $76.7$\,s on our real data). If it lies on the inherited
surface, the copy refines it. Static classes (bed, desk, wardrobe) are excluded:
their session copies legitimately add views that the previous session never
saw. The test runs every round, at session end, and decides what is displayed
(two poses are never united).

\subsection{Background Geometry}
\label{sec:background}

The inherited background mesh is registered into the live mesh of the new
session. Its vertices are deleted only where the session's rays confirm absence
(the same seen-through evidence, at the ray verificator's depth band); vertices
the session never re-observes stay as memory; the session's own reconstruction
is added. This is a conservative union: it never removes unobserved memory, and
it keeps a re-observed but slightly misaligned wall next to the session's own
copy (\cref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Persistent State Contract}
\label{sec:contract}

A session exports (i) the 4D map: one full scene snapshot per reconciliation
round, so the map answers queries at any past time; (ii) the fragment registry
with birth/death times and provenance; (iii) the pooled sensor statistics of
\cref{sec:absence}. The next session is an independent process; it receives the
export, a rigid transform between the two sessions' frames (from any
registration), and nothing else. All exported maps are packed losslessly
(delta-coded snapshots, $30\times$) and read back through the same interface.
