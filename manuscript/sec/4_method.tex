\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

A map update must decide which observations still describe the scene and which elements of the earlier map remain.
Both are questions about rays and object states, so our update layer answers them beside an existing mapper
(\cref{fig:method}) with three decisions. \emph{State intervals} record when each object
state holds (\cref{sec:states}). \emph{Valid domains} restrict each observation to the space where the state it
measured still holds (\cref{sec:validity}). The \emph{continuation test} keeps an element of the previous map until
current observations place its surface elsewhere (\cref{sec:memory}). A mapper connects to the layer through the
\emph{update interface} and keeps its own integration of measured surfaces (\cref{sec:representations}). Every element keeps its \emph{lifetime},
so the map of any earlier time remains available, and each run hands its state to the next (\cref{sec:handoff}).

\subsection{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

We address persistent map updating. An existing mapper builds a map from one run through an indoor scene, and later
runs revisit the scene after it has changed. The layer targets a map that is correct at every time: the map at time $t$ shows
the surfaces that held at $t$, and a region that a run leaves unobserved keeps its last observed state. We target
surfaces that disappear, appear, or move. Each change may happen in view, out of view during a run, or between runs. Moving people are masked out of
the static map.

Like Panoptic Multi-TSDFs~\cite{schmid2022panoptic}, the layer starts from posed, segmented RGB-D
frames. An observation
$o$ is the depth ray of one pixel with depth, with measured range $r_o$, time $t_o$, and segment label
$\ell_o$, possibly none. Each physical object $\ell$ has a sequence of geometric states. A state $s$ has a
reconstructed surface $G_s$ and a state interval $[b_s, d_s)$, and ending a state moves it to history. The map
consists of elements $\mathcal{E}$ that the mapper can keep or end individually: a surface face of a TSDF mesh, a 3D
Gaussian, or an occupied cell. Element $e$ has a position $x_e$, a half-size $h_e$ (half a voxel, three standard
deviations of a Gaussian, or half a cell), an identity $\ell_e$ that is $0$ for the background, and a lifetime
$[c_e, d_e)$. The map at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}(t) = \{\, e \in \mathcal{E} : c_e \le t < d_e \,\}.
  \label{eq:map_at_t}
\end{equation}
An element lives from the change-detection round (\cref{sec:states}) that first shows it, $c_e$, to the time it ends,
$d_e$, and an object element of
state $s$ has $d_e = d_s$. Ending an element sets $d_e$ instead of deleting it, so the map of any earlier time remains available.

\subsection{State Intervals: One Likelihood, One Prior, One Decision}
\label{sec:states}

Objects change in three ways: they move in view, change out of view during a run, or change between runs. The layer
decides all three with one likelihood, one prior, and one decision rule, and the three differ only in their evidence
and in the odds they start from. The decisions run in periodic change-detection rounds and yield the interval $[b_s, d_s)$ of every
state. Every state begins at its first observation. Its last support is the last depth image that supports its
surface. A state ended by a successor closes at the round that decides the handover, or at its last support if that
comes later, so the map shows both states until the handover. A state ended without a successor closes midway between
its last support and the deciding round.

\paragraph{Evidence.}
The layer reconstructs object states from the segmented depth, detects moving people with the free-space cue of
Dynablox~\cite{schmid2023dynablox}, as Khronos does~\cite{schmid2024khronos}, keeps them out of the static map, and
tests each state against later depth images. A surface sample of the state is \emph{supported} if the reading at its
pixel lies within 5\,cm of its depth, \emph{seen through} if the reading lies farther, and \emph{occluded} if it lies
closer. Readings more than $60^\circ$ from the sample normal do not count~\cite{nguyen2012kinect}. Only reliable
samples vote, which excludes thin parts, edges, and mislabeled pixels (supplementary material), and a depth image is
scored only if it judges at least $\min(30, \nu_{\mathrm{rel}})$ reliable samples, $\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}$ being the number of
reliable samples of the state.

\paragraph{Three explanations.}
Let $A$ be the current state of object $\ell$ and $N$ the observations of $\ell$ in the current run that the layer has
not yet associated with $A$. Under $S$, $A$ persists and $N$ shows other parts of it. Under $M$, $A$ has ended and $N$
is its successor. Under $U$, $A$ persists and $N$ is spurious: a fragment or a mislabel. Occluded readings, readings outside the view,
and invalid readings have likelihood ratio one, and the remaining readings fall into three disjoint groups.

The first group, the \emph{old-site evidence}, consists of the readings that fall on $G_A$ after its last support. Depth image
$i$ judges $n_i$ reliable samples and sees through $k_i$ of them, with fraction $f_i = k_i/n_i$. Under $S$, $f$ follows a
Beta density $p_A$ that the layer learns from the object's own in-place observations, and under $M$, $f$ is uniform. A
Page statistic~\cite{page1954cusum} maximizes the evidence over the first depth image $j$ after the change:
\begin{equation}
  L_t = \max\Big(0,\ \max_{j \le t} \sum_{i=j}^{t} w_i(j)\,\big[-\log p_A(f_i)\big]\Big), \qquad
  w_i(j) = \frac{\nu_i(j)}{\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}},
  \label{eq:absence}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_i(j)$ counts the reliable samples that image $i$ judges for the first time since image $j$ and
$\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}$ counts all reliable samples. The weights of a window sum to at most one, so each sample testifies
once and repeated looks at a small patch cannot add up to a whole object.

The second group, the \emph{displacement evidence}, is the position of $N$ relative to $G_A$. Let $\theta$ be the share
of $N$'s surface that lies farther than 10\,cm from $G_A$. Under $S$, $N$ may show unseen parts of $A$ at any distance,
so $\theta$ follows a Jeffreys prior. Under $M$, it follows that prior restricted to $\theta > 1/2$. The Bayes factor
is then $B_{M:S} = 2P(\theta > 1/2 \mid N) \in [0, 2]$. The third group, the \emph{new-site evidence}, consists of the observations of $N$'s own surface, whose
absence likelihood ratios, computed as for $G_A$, multiply to $\Lambda_U$, the likelihood ratio of $U$ against a genuine
$N$. Visible motion, flagged by the layer's object tracker as in Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos}, has likelihood ratio
$\infty$ for $M$, so the same rule ends the state at once.

\paragraph{One prior.}
A test of $\ell$ resolves when a round ends one of its states or confirms that it continues, and the prior $q_\ell$, the
only one the layer learns, is the probability that a resolved test of $\ell$ ends in a change. It is the posterior mean of a Beta distribution whose pseudo-counts $\alpha_c, \beta_c$ come from the other
objects of $\ell$'s class and whose counts are the $m_\ell$ resolved tests of $\ell$ that ended in a change and the $u_\ell$ that did not:
\begin{equation}
  q_\ell = \frac{\alpha_c + m_\ell}{\alpha_c + \beta_c + m_\ell + u_\ell}.
  \label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
We write $q$ for $q_\ell$ below.

\paragraph{One decision rule.}
Every action minimizes the expected loss under two losses. Showing a surface costs the area of the symmetric difference
between the shown and the true current surface, which puts the handover at $P(M) > 1/2$. When no successor is
observed, wrongly ending a state costs 99 times more than wrongly keeping it, which puts the end of a state at odds of
99. Given the displacement and new-site evidence, and with fixed even odds, $v = 1/2$, that $N$ is genuine,
the three explanations receive the weights
\begin{equation}
  P(S) \propto (1-q)\,v, \qquad P(M) \propto q\,v\,B_{M:S}, \qquad P(U) \propto (1-v)\,\Lambda_U,
  \label{eq:smu}
\end{equation}
and $N$ replaces $A$ when $P(M) > 1/2$, that is, $q\,B_{M:S} > (1-q) + \Lambda_U (1-v)/v$. A handover thus needs
$q > (1 + \Lambda_U)/3$. The old-site evidence decides the end of a state without a successor. A state begun in the run
ends when $L_t > \ln 99$, from even odds, and an inherited state when $\max(L_t,\ \operatorname{logit} q +
L^{\mathrm{pre}}_t) > \ln 99$, where $L^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$ is the sum in \cref{eq:absence} from the state's first scored
image in the run, without the clamp at zero. In both cases, the images of that window must see through more samples of
$G_A$ than they support. The prior thus enters every handover and the end of an inherited state. Merging and display use
the same weights (supplementary material). \Cref{tab:decisions} lists the evidence and the rule for each kind of
change.

\subsection{Valid Domains}
\label{sec:validity}

An observation is \emph{temporally valid} only where the state it measured still holds, and it shapes the current
map only there. The layer removes from the fusion each labeled pixel of $\ell$ whose time lies
outside the interval $[b_s, d_s)$ of $\ell$'s current state $s$, and all of them if $\ell$ has no current state, so an
earlier pose of $\ell$ cannot reappear. Every other pixel keeps
its measured surface, but its free space ends a truncation band $T$ before $\lambda_o$, the first intersection of
its ray with a current object, taken from the object's faces or, where they are missing, from the voxels of the object's
own readings, and $\lambda_o = \infty$ if the ray meets none. With $\rho_o(x)$ the range of $x$, let
$\delta_o(x) = r_o - \rho_o(x)$ be the signed distance of $x$ to the measured surface along the ray, positive in front of
it. The valid domain of observation $o$ at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_o(t) = \{\, x : \delta_o(x) > -T \,\wedge\, \big(\delta_o(x) \le T \,\vee\, \rho_o(x) < \lambda_o - T\big) \,\},
  \label{eq:domain}
\end{equation}
and empty for a removed pixel. For a mapper that optimizes its elements, the valid domain of a keyframe is the set of
elements it may train. The domain depends on $t$ through the state intervals, which change at every
change-detection round, and the layer uses only observations received up to $t$.

\subsection{The Continuation Test}
\label{sec:memory}

The continuation test keeps the map persistent: an element of the previous map continues until observations of the
current run, within their valid domains, place its surface elsewhere. For element $e$ and an observation $o$ whose pixel
sees $x_e$ within its valid domain, let $\tau_e(\rho) = \max(h_e, \sigma(\rho), \tau_{\min})$, where
$\sigma(\rho)$ is the depth residual of the current reconstruction at range $\rho$, smoothed across range bins, and
$\tau_{\min}$ is a floor that the mapper sets, zero unless stated. The observation \emph{supports} $e$ if $|\delta_o(x_e)| \le \tau_e$
and \emph{sees through} $e$ if $\delta_o(x_e) > \tau_e$, and a reading in front of $e$ casts no direct vote. Let $n_{\mathrm{sup}}$ and
$n_{\mathrm{thr}}$ count the supports and see-throughs over the current run. Direct evidence decides by majority. Without
a majority, $e$ ends only if it lies behind a second layer of the same surface within the truncation band $T$
(condition $C(e)$). Otherwise, it keeps its prior:
\begin{equation}
  K(e) = \begin{cases}
    [\,n_{\mathrm{sup}} > n_{\mathrm{thr}}\,], & n_{\mathrm{sup}} \ne n_{\mathrm{thr}},\\
    0, & n_{\mathrm{sup}} = n_{\mathrm{thr}},\ C(e),\\
    K_{\mathrm{prior}}(e), & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:continue}
\end{equation}
An element that the previous map shows has $K_{\mathrm{prior}} = 1$, so an unobserved region keeps the earlier map. If
the direct votes are independent and each points the right way with probability $p > 1/2$, the log-likelihood ratio of removal against
continuation is $(n_{\mathrm{thr}} - n_{\mathrm{sup}})\log[p/(1-p)]$, so the majority is the maximum-likelihood
decision for every such $p$. The test $C(e)$ encodes a property of the representation: a truncation band holds one surface of a
given orientation, so an element behind such a layer duplicates it. Object elements follow their state: the elements of
an ended state leave the current map at once and stay in history.

\subsection{The Update Interface}
\label{sec:representations}

The update interface has four operations: the mapper reports its elements, ends the elements the layer names, accepts
the state intervals, and integrates observations within their valid domains. The mapper supplies the half-size $h_e$
of its elements, the floor $\tau_{\min}$, its truncation band $T$, and its test $C(e)$, and the layer makes the state
decisions and runs the continuation test with the same code for all three mappers. Each mapper keeps its own integration
of measured surfaces: the TSDF its weighted average, GaME its photometric and depth optimization, and wavemap its log-odds integration of occupied space. We call a mapper connected through
this interface a \emph{backend}. The supplementary material details each backend.

\paragraph{TSDF backend.}
Our TSDF backend is a Khronos pipeline~\cite{schmid2024khronos} that keeps Khronos' active window, tracking, and object
extraction. Khronos' projective integrator fuses each frame within its valid domain into one TSDF with 5\,cm voxels
and truncation $T = 10$\,cm by the weighted running average~\cite{curless1996volumetric,newcombe2011kinectfusion},
\begin{equation}
  F^{+}(x) = \frac{W(x)\,F(x) + \omega_o(x)\,\phi_o(x)}{W(x) + \omega_o(x)},\quad
  W^{+}(x) = \min\big(W(x) + \omega_o(x),\, W_{\max}\big),\quad x \in \Omega_o(t),
  \label{eq:tsdf}
\end{equation}
with Khronos' signed distance $\phi_o$ and weight $\omega_o$. When a round changes the state intervals, the backend
re-integrates the affected blocks, and Khronos' mesh integrator extracts the present surface. Background faces of the
previous map pass \cref{eq:continue}, where $C(e)$ holds if a face lies behind a layer of the present surface within $T$
and both face the same way, and object faces follow their state. Background faces at the old site of an ended state leave
with it, and faces of a continuing inherited state that lie inside its present surface leave at once. The current map is the present surface, its gaps filled with archived background
faces of the active window, together with the continuing faces.

\paragraph{Gaussian backend.}
Our Gaussian backend is GaME~\cite{yugay2026game}, which optimizes 3D Gaussians~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians} over
keyframes with photometric and depth losses, and FlashSplat~\cite{shen2024flashsplat} assigns object identities to the
Gaussians. Each Gaussian carries a lifetime, and a keyframe at time $t_k$ renders and trains only
$\mathcal{M}(t_k)$, so the observations of an ended state train only the Gaussians of their time. At each keyframe of
the current run, the backend tests every live Gaussian of the previous map at the Gaussian's own pixel, with $\tau_{\min} = 5$\,cm. A reading within
$T = 10$\,cm in front of the Gaussian, from the side from which the previous run saw it, \emph{covers} the Gaussian. The test $C(e)$ holds if the run neither supports nor
sees through the Gaussian and covering readings outnumber the readings farther in front. Each keyframe $k$ trains only its
valid domain $V_k \subseteq \mathcal{M}(t_k)$: for a keyframe of the current run, the Gaussians that the run created,
supports, or covers, and for an earlier keyframe, those that the current run never covers. An earlier Gaussian leaves
the map only by \cref{eq:continue} or with its ended object state. The refinement after the last run trains each Gaussian only with the keyframes whose valid domain
contains it and adds no Gaussians.

\paragraph{Occupancy backend.}
Our occupancy backend is wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap}, which accumulates log-odds occupancy in a
wavelet-compressed octree with clamping~\cite{hornung2013octomap}. Its elements are the occupied cells that contain a
measured surface point. Wavemap integrates the occupied space of every frame, and the layer takes over the clearing of
occupied cells: a cell leaves the current map only through \cref{eq:continue} or with its ended object state, and an ended
cell keeps its lifetime for the maps of earlier times. Cells carry no orientation, so $C(e)$ never holds and a tie keeps
the prior.

\subsection{Run Handoff and Implementation}
\label{sec:handoff}

Each run exports the map timeline with element lifetimes and the pooled sensor statistics of the old-site evidence. It also
exports the inference state of the objects (their current states, intervals, and surfaces), which seeds the next run's
decisions, and the shown surface of its final map, which becomes the next run's memory. The next run loads this state,
aligns its frames to the common frame, and runs the same decisions. All datasets and backends share the constants: the
5\,cm support tolerance, the $60^\circ$ incidence limit, the $\ln 99$ threshold, at least $\min(30, \nu_{\mathrm{rel}})$
judged samples per depth image, and the 10\,cm displacement distance. The truncation band $T$ is set per backend.
