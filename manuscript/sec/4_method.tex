\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Our goal is a map that stays correct while the scene changes. The input is a posed RGB-D stream with SAM
masks~\cite{kirillov2023sam}, which assign each pixel to an object and keep that assignment across frames. When an object moves, the map should drop
it from its old place and rebuild it where it now stands. Static regions should keep accumulating observations, regions
out of view should keep their last observed geometry, and the map should still show the scene at any earlier time. An
object may change in view, while the camera looks elsewhere, or while the robot is away between two visits. We handle
all of these cases with one mechanism in two parts. The first judges which object states have ended and which have
begun (\cref{sec:states}). The second applies these judgments to the map: it ends the geometry of ended states,
restricts every observation to the scene it measured, and decides which parts of the earlier map remain
(\cref{sec:validity}). Both parts reason about object states and the times of observations, not about voxels,
Gaussians, or cells. The same mechanism therefore updates a TSDF, a 3D Gaussian map, and an occupancy map, each of
which keeps its own way of integrating a measurement (\cref{sec:representations,fig:method}).

\subsection{Map and Object States}
\label{sec:setting}

An observation $o$ is the depth reading of one pixel, with measured range $r_o$, capture time $t_o$, and instance label
$\ell_o$. Each object $\ell$ is described by a sequence of static states. A state $s$ has a surface $G_s$, reconstructed
from the observations of $\ell$ during the state, and an interval $[b_s, d_s)$ in which it holds. When an object moves,
its next state is reconstructed from the observations at its new place alone, so no geometry is carried over from the
old place. The map is a set of elements $\mathcal{E}$: the surface faces of a TSDF, the Gaussians of a Gaussian map, or
the occupied cells of an occupancy map. Element $e$ has a position $x_e$, a half-size $h_e$ (half a voxel, three
standard deviations of a Gaussian, or half a cell), an instance label $\ell_e$, which is $0$ for the background, and a
lifetime $[c_e, d_e)$. An element of state $s$ ends with it, $d_e = d_s$. The map at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}(t) = \{\, e \in \mathcal{E} : c_e \le t < d_e \,\}.
  \label{eq:map_at_t}
\end{equation}
Ending an element sets $d_e$ instead of deleting it, so the map of every earlier time remains available.

\subsection{Judging Change}
\label{sec:states}

A camera sees only part of the scene at a time, and most surfaces are out of view or occluded most of the time. A
surface that is not observed is not evidence that it is gone, and a surface seen through at one small patch is not
evidence that the whole object has left. We therefore judge a change only from readings that reach the place of an
existing surface, and we count them by how much of the surface they cover.

\paragraph{Evidence from the current depth.}
We test each state against every later depth image. Each reliable surface sample of $G_s$ is projected into the image.
Reliable samples exclude thin parts, edges, and mislabeled pixels, whose readings pass the surface even while it is in
place. A sample is
\emph{supported} if the reading at its pixel lies within a sensor tolerance of its depth, \emph{seen through} if the
reading lies farther, and \emph{occluded} if it lies closer. Only supported and seen-through samples carry evidence.
Occluded samples, samples outside the view, and missing readings carry none, so a surface that the camera does not see
stays as it is.

\paragraph{From samples to the whole object.}
Depth image $i$ judges $n_i$ samples of $G_s$ and sees through $k_i$ of them. Even a surface that is still in place is
seen through at a small fraction $f_i = k_i/n_i$, because of depth noise and slight misregistration. We model this
fraction by a Beta density $p_s$ learned from the in-place observations of the object, drawn toward those of all objects
while the object has few of its own, so the test adapts to the sensor and to the object. A change makes $f$ uniform. A Page
statistic~\cite{page1954cusum} accumulates the evidence from the first image $j$ after the change:
\begin{equation}
  L_t = \max\Big(0,\ \max_{j \le t} \sum_{i=j}^{t} w_i(j)\,\big[-\log p_s(f_i)\big]\Big), \qquad
  w_i(j) = \frac{\nu_i(j)}{\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}},
  \label{eq:absence}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_i(j)$ counts the reliable samples that image $i$ judges for the first time since image $j$, and
$\nu_{\mathrm{rel}}$ counts all reliable samples of the state. The weights of a window sum to at most one, so each
sample testifies once. Looking repeatedly at a small patch therefore cannot add up to the whole object, which keeps the
decision accurate, and the state ends as soon as $L_t$ crosses its threshold, without waiting until the whole surface
has been seen. Its interval closes midway between its last support and that decision.

\paragraph{Successors.}
New observations of an object at another place raise a different question: they may show more of the current state, a
successor after a move, or a spurious fragment such as a mislabeled region. We weigh the three explanations with the
share of the new surface that lies away from the current one, which favors a move, the depth evidence on the new surface
itself, which exposes a fragment that later readings see through, and the prior probability $q_\ell$ that object $\ell$
changes. The prior is estimated from the resolved decisions of $\ell$ and of the objects of its class. When a move is
more probable than not, the new observations begin the successor state and the current state ends; until then, they
remain a candidate.

\paragraph{Motion in view and changes between visits.}
An object that moves in view is followed by the object tracker, as in Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos}. Its state ends
when the motion starts, the object is a trajectory while it moves, and the frames after it settles reconstruct its next
state. People are detected with the free-space cue of Dynablox~\cite{schmid2023dynablox}, as in Khronos, and kept out
of the static map. A later visit starts from the states that the previous visit saved (\cref{sec:handoff}). A change
made while the robot was away is judged by the same tests, and an inherited state begins its test from the prior odds
$q_\ell$ that its object has changed.

\subsection{Applying Change}
\label{sec:validity}

A decision alone does not update a map. After a chair moves, the frames taken before still show it at its old place and
would fuse it back, the free space observed before a new object arrived would carve into the new object, and earlier
keyframes would train a Gaussian map toward the old scene. Discarding the earlier observations or the earlier map would
remove these conflicts but also everything they still describe correctly. The second part of the mechanism therefore
applies each decision to all the geometry it concerns and lets every observation act only on the scene it measured.

\paragraph{Ending a state ends its geometry.}
When a state ends, every element of that state ends with it, including parts that no reading has seen through, such as
the back of a moved sofa. These elements leave the current map and remain in the maps of earlier times
(\cref{eq:map_at_t}).

\paragraph{Valid domains.}
An observation is correct for the scene at the time it was taken. A depth image of a chair taken before the chair moved
still describes the floor and the wall around it, but no longer the chair. An image of empty floor taken before a box
was put there describes free space that the box now occupies. We therefore restrict each observation to its
\emph{valid domain}, the space where the state it measured still holds. A pixel of object $\ell$ taken outside the
interval of $\ell$'s current state, or any pixel of $\ell$ once $\ell$ has no current state, is excluded from the
integration of the current map. Every other pixel keeps its measured surface, but
its free space ends a band $T$ before $\lambda_o$, the range at which its ray first meets the surface of a current
object, with $\lambda_o = \infty$ if it meets none. With $\rho_o(x)$ the range of point $x$ along the ray and
$\delta_o(x) = r_o - \rho_o(x)$ its signed distance to the measured surface, positive in front of it, the valid domain
of $o$ at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_o(t) = \{\, x : \delta_o(x) > -T \,\wedge\, \big(\delta_o(x) \le T \,\vee\, \rho_o(x) < \lambda_o - T\big) \,\},
  \label{eq:domain}
\end{equation}
and empty for an excluded pixel. For a map that is optimized over keyframes, the same rule is applied in time: the
keyframe taken at $t_k$ renders and trains only $\mathcal{M}(t_k)$. An earlier keyframe thus keeps shaping the geometry
of its own time and of the scene that has not changed, and it no longer pulls the current map toward the old scene.
Valid domains change whenever a state interval changes, and the map at $t$ uses only the observations received up to
$t$.

\paragraph{Keeping the earlier map.}
\label{sec:memory}
An element of the earlier map that belongs to no ended state remains until the observations of the current visit place
its surface elsewhere. For element $e$ and an observation $o$ whose pixel sees $x_e$ within its valid domain, let
$\tau_e = \max(h_e, \sigma(\rho_o(x_e)), \tau_{\min})$, where $\sigma(\rho)$ is the depth residual of the current
reconstruction at range $\rho$ and $\tau_{\min}$ is a floor, zero unless stated. The observation supports $e$ if $|\delta_o(x_e)| \le \tau_e$ and sees through $e$ if
$\delta_o(x_e) > \tau_e$. A reading in front of $e$ casts no vote, since the element may be occluded. With
$n_{\mathrm{sup}}$ and $n_{\mathrm{thr}}$ the supports and see-throughs over the current visit, the element is kept with
\begin{equation}
  K(e) = \begin{cases}
    [\,n_{\mathrm{sup}} > n_{\mathrm{thr}}\,], & n_{\mathrm{sup}} \ne n_{\mathrm{thr}},\\
    0, & n_{\mathrm{sup}} = n_{\mathrm{thr}},\ C(e),\\
    1, & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:continue}
\end{equation}
If every vote points the right way with the same probability above one half, the majority is the maximum-likelihood
decision. The condition $C(e)$ holds when a surface that the current visit measured lies in front of $e$ within the band
$T$ and faces the same way. A band holds one surface of a given orientation, so $e$ is then a stale copy of that surface
and makes way for the new estimate. Any other element remains, so a region that the current visit does not observe
keeps the earlier map.

\subsection{Three Maps}
\label{sec:representations}

Each map keeps its own integration of measurements. The mechanism changes which observations it integrates, through the
valid domains, which elements it shows, through their lifetimes, and which earlier elements remain, through
\cref{eq:continue}.

\paragraph{TSDF.}
Our TSDF map builds on Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos} and keeps its active window, tracking, and object extraction.
Khronos' projective integrator fuses each frame within its valid domain into a TSDF with 5\,cm voxels and truncation
$T = 10$\,cm by the weighted running average~\cite{curless1996volumetric,newcombe2011kinectfusion},
\begin{equation}
  F^{+}(x) = \frac{W(x)\,F(x) + \omega_o(x)\,\phi_o(x)}{W(x) + \omega_o(x)},\quad
  W^{+}(x) = \min\big(W(x) + \omega_o(x),\, W_{\max}\big),\quad x \in \Omega_o(t),
  \label{eq:tsdf}
\end{equation}
with Khronos' signed distance $\phi_o$ and weight $\omega_o$. When a decision changes a state interval, the blocks whose
valid domains changed are integrated again from the stored frames in their order, and Khronos' mesh integrator extracts
the surface. Its elements are the surface faces. Faces of the earlier map continue by \cref{eq:continue}, where $C(e)$
compares a face with the surface extracted in the current visit. The current map is that surface together with the
continuing faces of the earlier map.

\paragraph{3D Gaussians.}
Our Gaussian map builds on GaME~\cite{yugay2026game}, which optimizes 3D Gaussians~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians} over
keyframes with photometric and depth losses, and FlashSplat~\cite{shen2024flashsplat} assigns the instance labels to the
Gaussians. Each Gaussian carries a lifetime, and the keyframe taken at $t_k$ renders and trains only $\mathcal{M}(t_k)$.
Within it, a keyframe of the current visit trains the Gaussians that the visit created, supports, or covers, and an
earlier keyframe trains those that the current visit has not covered. Keyframes of the current visit test each Gaussian
of the earlier map at the Gaussian's own pixel by \cref{eq:continue}, with $\tau_{\min} = 5$\,cm. A Gaussian that
GaME's opacity pruning removes is ended instead of deleted, so the maps of earlier times keep it. The refinement after
the last visit trains each Gaussian only with the keyframes whose valid domain contains it.

\paragraph{Occupancy.}
Our occupancy map builds on wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap}, which accumulates log-odds occupancy in a
wavelet-compressed octree with clamping~\cite{hornung2013octomap}. Its own update lowers the occupancy of the cells a
reading passes through, so a cell that the current visit sees through leaves the map without a vote. Its elements are
the occupied cells that contain a measured surface point. The cells of an ended state end together. Where the current
visit first measures an object surface in a cell that an earlier visit observed as free, the free-space evidence of the
earlier visit is withdrawn from that cell, which limits the old free space as \cref{eq:domain} does, and the new
surface is integrated without having to overcome it. An earlier cell for which $C(e)$ holds ends as a stale copy. Ending
a cell sets its lifetime, so the maps of earlier times keep it.

\subsection{Across Visits and Implementation}
\label{sec:handoff}

At the end of a visit, we save the map with the lifetimes of its elements, the states of all objects with their
intervals and surfaces, and the pooled sensor statistics of the absence test. The next visit loads them, aligns its
frames to the common frame, and continues with the same decisions. All datasets and maps share the constants of the
tests: the 5\,cm support tolerance, a $60^\circ$ incidence limit~\cite{nguyen2012kinect}, the threshold $\ln 99$ on
$L_t$, at least $\min(30, \nu_{\mathrm{rel}})$ judged samples per depth image, and the 10\,cm displacement distance.
The band $T$ is the truncation of each map.
