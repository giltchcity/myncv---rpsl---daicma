\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A map can become outdated while it is still being built. A robot may observe a chair being moved,
discover its absence when it looks back, or return to a rearranged room (\cref{fig:teaser}).
All of these changes must be incorporated into the same reconstruction. Since the robot sees only part
of the scene at a time, updating changed geometry must also preserve valid geometry outside the current
view. Such maps support robots that repeatedly navigate and interact with a space, as well as visual
models used to inspect its evolving appearance.

Khronos introduced the first real-time system for metric-semantic reconstruction of a scene over time~\cite{schmid2024khronos}.
GaME introduced the first mapping system with novel-view synthesis for evolving, long-term dynamic
scenes~\cite{yugay2026game}. wavemap combines state-of-the-art lossless occupancy compression with
efficient multi-resolution integration~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap}. These advances provide strong
reconstruction capabilities across different representations. Scene changes pose a shared problem for
them: how should new evidence revise the accumulated geometry and measurements?

After a chair moves, earlier images still show it at its old location, while earlier depth rays may mark
its new location as free space. These measurements were accurate when acquired; the scene they describe has since changed.
Continuing to apply these observations without accounting for the change
can restore outdated geometry or interfere with the new reconstruction. Yet the same images and depth
measurements can still describe the unchanged wall. A change decision therefore needs to revise both the stored geometry and the observations
that continue to influence it.

We use scene-state intervals as a common basis for geometry and observation updates. A confirmed change
ends the old state's geometry in the current map and revises where earlier measurements can act.
For an earlier depth image of the wall, this retains the wall measurement while stopping its free-space
update before a newly placed object. New observations reconstruct the changed scene, while compatible
earlier measurements continue to complete it. The same state decision thus governs what the map contains
and which evidence can shape it.

We introduce \ours, a unified dynamic mapping method with two components, \emph{change detection} and
\emph{map update} (\cref{fig:method}). Change detection checks retained surfaces against current depth
and combines this evidence with motion cues to determine which states continue, end, or begin.
Map update applies these decisions to geometry and observation contributions. The same procedure continues
when observations resume on another visit and governs TSDF fusion, Gaussian optimization, and occupancy
integration. Geometry that is occluded or left unobserved is retained until evidence supports an update;
ended states remain available for queries at earlier times.

Our real and synthetic sequences contain changes both within and across visits in the same evolving map.
Flat and 3RScan provide further cross-visit evaluation. Across these four datasets, all three representations
improve geometry, object reconstruction, and change detection over their corresponding baselines
(\cref{tab:main}). Partial revisits demonstrate greater completeness, and TESSE provides an additional
evaluation of temporal reconstruction. We examine Gaussian reconstruction in detail, with
0.96--3.43\,dB higher novel-view PSNR than GaME across the four datasets and ablations that measure the
effects of geometry retirement and observation reuse.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A unified dynamic mapping method that uses scene-state intervals to coordinate geometry and observation
  updates within and across visits, realized in TSDF, Gaussian, and occupancy reconstruction.
  \item An evaluation of continuous reconstruction under both types of change, with public benchmarks,
  temporal and completeness evaluations, and detailed Gaussian rendering results and mechanism ablations.
\end{itemize}
