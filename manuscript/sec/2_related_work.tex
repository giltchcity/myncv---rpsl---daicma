\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

We organize prior work according to the two-level temporal structure of our
problem. D1 and D2 are complementary observation modes inside one continuously
running dynamic-mapping session: D1 models motion while it is observed, whereas
D2 infers a change revealed only after the robot revisits a region. D3 connects
completed independent sessions through persistent scene memory.

\subsection{Single-session dynamic mapping: D1 and D2}

Dense object-level SLAM has developed increasingly expressive D1
representations. Co-Fusion maintains independent surfel models for a static
background and multiple rigid objects~\cite{runz2017cofusion}. MID-Fusion uses
object-level octree volumes and estimates geometric, semantic, and motion
properties~\cite{xu2019midfusion}, while EM-Fusion combines local signed-distance
volumes with probabilistic data association and occlusion
reasoning~\cite{strecke2019emfusion}. Feature-based systems emphasize joint state
estimation: DynaSLAM~II tightly couples camera, object trajectories, and scene
structure~\cite{bescos2021dynaslam2}, and DynoSAM provides factor-graph
formulations for joint robot and object motion estimation~\cite{morris2025dynosam}.
DYNEMO-SLAM incorporates moving agents and displaced objects into an optimizable
3D scene graph, but evaluates a marker-based continuous-run setting and leaves
real perception and data association outside its scope~\cite{giberna2025dynemo}.

A larger robustness literature, including DynaSLAM and DYMRO-SLAM, treats dynamic
content primarily as interference. Semantic masks, geometric checks, or optical
flow are used to exclude dynamic features so that camera localization and a
static map remain reliable. Such methods are relevant localization baselines but
do not preserve moving entities as D1 history.

Non-rigid and 4D reconstruction broadens D1 beyond rigid objects. Classical
systems maintain a canonical surface and a time-varying deformation model,
whereas recent primitive and Gaussian methods preserve richer temporal geometry
from a continuous video. Persistence in this setting commonly denotes object
permanence within one sequence rather than state transfer between independent
mapping sessions.

D2 requires reasoning beyond frame-to-frame tracking. Bore \etal model movable
objects with linked local-motion and global-jump processes while the robot
observes only part of a large map~\cite{bore2019movable}. Changing-SLAM combines
dynamic-feature filtering with a Bayesian object-persistence map and long-term
association inside ORB-SLAM3~\cite{soares2023changing}. Khronos provides the
closest dense D1+D2 formulation: an active temporal window tracks visible
motion, whereas a global process reconciles fragments separated by observation
gaps using spatio-temporal and ray-based evidence~\cite{schmid2024khronos}.

SuperMap addresses continuous-session semantic-object maintenance rather than
cross-session scene-memory restoration. It associates open-vocabulary instances,
recursively updates object-point occupancy and semantic confidence, and builds a
queryable scene graph~\cite{zhao2026supermap}. Its evaluated change run lasts ten
minutes and contains object additions and removals. The method is relevant to
instance association and object-level D2-like updates, but its own limitations
report weak tracking of highly dynamic objects, and it does not preserve dense
D1 trajectories or define a process-separated D3 protocol.

\subsection{Gaussian and neural 4D representations}

Gaussian methods cover several distinct objectives. WildGS-SLAM represents the
dynamic-robust/static-map branch~\cite{zheng2025wildgsslam}. 4DTAM jointly
estimates camera motion and non-rigid surface evolution~\cite{matsuki2025fourdtam},
4D Gaussian Splatting SLAM separates static and deformable primitives for D1
reconstruction~\cite{li2025fourdgsslam}, and DynaGSLAM emphasizes real-time
tracking and prediction of observed moving objects~\cite{li2026dynagslam}.
GaME revises one online Gaussian map after out-of-view changes and is therefore
most relevant to D2~\cite{yugay2026game}. LT-Gaussian updates an old Gaussian map
with a later LiDAR traversal and is a D3 map-revision component rather than a
complete dynamic SLAM system~\cite{cheng2025ltgaussian}. These methods are
important representation references, although their rendering-oriented outputs
do not form direct baselines for metric-semantic mesh and scene-memory metrics.

\subsection{Cross-session current-map maintenance: D3}

A major lifelong-mapping family aims to maintain a clean, current map for
localization. Efficient Long-Term Mapping uses a pose graph whose nodes contain
2D local point clouds~\cite{lazaro2018efficient}. Inside a session, consecutive
scans are aligned and merged with free-space-aware range rules that suppress
traces from moving people. Across sessions, inter-session loop closure triggers
local-map fusion, outdated-node removal, and graph-coherence preservation. It is
a genuine multi-session current-map updater, but it neither estimates D1 object
motion nor represents hidden D2 transitions.

Dynamic Pose Graph SLAM edits outdated scans after detected environmental
changes~\cite{walcott2012dynamic}. Pomerleau \etal maintain point-wise
static/dynamic probabilities and local velocities over repeated LiDAR
surveys~\cite{pomerleau2014longterm}. LT-Mapper separates multi-session alignment,
dynamic-object removal, and positive/negative map change management~\cite{kim2022ltmapper},
while RBIF uses probabilistic ray-bundle/voxel interference for conservative map
updates~\cite{breitfuss2024rbif}. These systems maintain localization geometry,
not complete dynamic-scene histories.

ELite estimates local ephemerality from rays to remove dynamic points in each new
LiDAR session and recursively updates point-wise global ephemerality in a
lifelong map~\cite{gil2025elite}. Its local/global hierarchy concerns
current-session dynamic removal and cross-session point transiency; it is not the
D1/D2 decomposition used here, because moving-object history is discarded before
the lifelong update.

ProbPer-LiLo also targets the static part of the environment~\cite{ali2026probperlilo}.
It models map features or semantic clusters as binary static/non-static
variables, uses single-session observations to detect moving content and
multi-map temporal consistency to identify quasi-static content, and removes
both classes. The resulting prior and current static maps are compared as
semantic voxel grids and classified into previously explored, newly explored,
co-existing, obsolete, and emerging regions. Its experiments recursively update
one lifelong map with multiple later traversals, making it a strong D3
static-map-refinement baseline. It does not preserve D1 history, infer D2 hidden
transitions, or maintain object identity beyond assumed cluster association.

\subsection{Cross-session object and volumetric state}

Object- and volume-centric methods retain richer D3 semantics. Panoptic
Multi-TSDFs initializes a later run from prior active and inactive volumetric
submaps and updates entities as persistent, absent, or
unobserved~\cite{schmid2022panoptic}. POCD maintains object-level volumes and
stationarity/change beliefs between traversals~\cite{qian2022pocd}, while
POV-SLAM jointly estimates robot pose and semi-static object consistency over
repeated visits~\cite{qian2023povslam}. ObVi-SLAM transfers selected persistent
object landmarks between deployments~\cite{adkins2024obvislam}. OASIS-Map
associates objects across a previous and current session and predicts appeared,
disappeared, moved, static, and unknown states~\cite{oh2026oasismap}; it remains
an under-review preprint at the time of writing.

\subsection{Predictive persistence models}

A complementary literature predicts recurring or semi-static state rather than
maintaining dense scene geometry. Occupancy-grid HMMs learn cell transition
probabilities~\cite{meyerdelius2012changinggrid}, FreMEn represents periodic
processes with compact frequency spectra~\cite{krajnik2017fremen}, and
Spatio-Temporal Hilbert Maps learn continuous time-conditioned occupancy
functions~\cite{senanayake2016sthm}. Perpetua models feature appearance and
disappearance with mixtures of persistence and emergence filters and maintains
multiple temporal hypotheses~\cite{saavedraruiz2025perpetua}. These methods are
not direct dense-SLAM baselines, but they establish persistence and temporal
prediction as existing mechanisms.

\subsection{Positioning of this work}

The relevant methods separate more sharply than broad terms such as
``dynamic,'' ``persistent,'' or ``lifelong'' suggest. Khronos provides dense
D1+D2 inference within one continuously running session. Efficient Long-Term
Mapping, ELite, ProbPer-LiLo, and related lifelong systems carry maps across
sessions but intentionally suppress or remove moving and quasi-static content to
produce an updated static representation. Panoptic Multi-TSDFs and POCD/POV-SLAM
retain richer object or volumetric D3 state but do not preserve the full D1
history of each session. Our target is the interface between these families: a
complete dense D1+D2 session exports object and structural scene state, an
independently started session reconciles D3 from that state, and the new session
again executes D1+D2 before producing the prior for the next deployment.
