\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Dynamic reconstruction.}
Co-Fusion, MID-Fusion, and EM-Fusion track objects and reconstruct their geometry during motion~\cite{runz2017cofusion,
xu2019midfusion,strecke2019emfusion}. DynaSLAM~II and DynoSAM jointly estimate camera and object
motion~\cite{bescos2021dynaslam2,morris2025dynosam}, while Dynablox detects objects entering observed free
space~\cite{schmid2023dynablox}. Dynamic Gaussian and surface representations capture time-varying geometry for
reconstruction and rendering~\cite{matsuki2025fourdtam,li2025fourdgsslam,li2026dynagslam}.
We use front-end motion cues together with changes discovered against a retained map, then apply the resulting
state changes through one map-update mechanism.

\paragraph{Updating evolving scenes.}
Map maintenance has been studied for point clouds~\cite{pomerleau2014longterm,lazaro2018efficient,kim2022ltmapper},
TSDFs~\cite{fehr2017tsdfchange,schmid2022panoptic}, occupancy grids~\cite{hornung2013octomap,reijgwart2023wavemap},
and Gaussians~\cite{yugay2026game,cheng2025ltgaussian,zeng2025gaussianupdate,ackermann2025clsplats}.
Panoptic Multi-TSDFs classifies object submaps as persistent, absent, or unobserved~\cite{schmid2022panoptic}.
POCD estimates object change and stationarity, and POV-SLAM couples this reasoning with pose
estimation~\cite{qian2022pocd,qian2023povslam}. Khronos reconstructs object fragments in an active window and uses
fragment reconciliation with a library of rays to infer their temporal presence~\cite{schmid2024khronos}.
GaME updates Gaussian geometry and masks conflicting regions of old keyframes~\cite{yugay2026game}.
wavemap integrates occupied and free-space evidence through clamped log odds~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap}.
Our method tests existing surfaces against current depth and uses the inferred state intervals to control both
geometry and observation contributions. The same mechanism drives these different native integration procedures.

\paragraph{Object association and scene history.}
Object-based maps associate observations across time and visits. RIO re-localizes instances in changing
scans~\cite{wald2019rio}; Living Scenes reconstructs objects from scans acquired at different
times~\cite{zhu2024livingscenes}; ObVi-SLAM and OASIS-Map transfer or associate object landmarks across
sessions~\cite{adkins2024obvislam,oh2026oasismap}. Other methods model the persistence of map content through
occupancy transitions~\cite{meyerdelius2012changinggrid}, periodic changes~\cite{krajnik2017fremen}, or multiple
appearance and disappearance hypotheses~\cite{saavedraruiz2025perpetua}. Khronos and CL-Splats also retain
information about earlier scene states~\cite{schmid2024khronos,ackermann2025clsplats}.
We connect temporal scene reasoning to reconstruction: an object's state interval controls which geometry is
current and which observations may update it. The stored intervals also support historical queries and continuation
on subsequent visits.
