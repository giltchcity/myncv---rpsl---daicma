\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

We organize prior work using the hierarchy of our problem rather than treating
all forms of scene change as three parallel categories. The first level is
\emph{single-session dynamic mapping}. Within one continuously running SLAM
session, D1 denotes motion observed as it occurs, whereas D2 denotes a change
that takes place while the robot is looking elsewhere and is inferred only when
the region is revisited. The second level is \emph{cross-session continuation}:
D3 denotes environmental evolution between completed independent sessions and
requires a later session to import persistent memory from an earlier one. Every
D3 session remains a full dynamic-mapping session and can contain new D1 and D2
events.

\subsection{Single-session visible dynamics (D1)}

A first family of methods models entities that move while they are continuously
observed. Co-Fusion maintains separate surfel models for the background and
multiple foreground objects and jointly segments, tracks, and reconstructs their
rigid motion from a live RGB-D stream~\cite{runz2017cofusion}. MaskFusion adds
instance-level semantic recognition and an object-aware map, but restricts its
tracked reconstruction to rigid objects; non-rigid objects such as people are
removed rather than reconstructed~\cite{runz2018maskfusion}. These systems retain
object-level geometry for observed motion but do not infer what happened after
an object left the active observation stream.

Other dynamic-scene systems prioritize a reliable static map. DynaSLAM combines
semantic and multi-view geometric masks to exclude moving or movable content
from tracking and mapping and reconstructs the background hidden by those
objects~\cite{bescos2018dynaslam}. Its output is deliberately a static reusable
map rather than a time-indexed history of moving entities. Dynablox detects
moving points by constructing a spatio-temporally conservative estimate of free
space inside an online volumetric map~\cite{schmid2023dynablox}; it is a motion
detector rather than an estimator of object pose and identity. DynoSAM instead
jointly optimizes camera state, dynamic-object motion or poses, and scene
structure in a factor-graph framework~\cite{morris2025dynosam}. These methods
establish strong D1 solutions but do not by themselves infer hidden
rearrangements during observation gaps.

\subsection{Single-session hidden changes and unified D1+D2 mapping}

D2 requires reasoning beyond frame-to-frame motion. Bore \etal model movable
objects in a large partially observed environment as undergoing frequent local
motion and rarer global jumps while the robot observes only part of the
map~\cite{bore2019movable}. Their linked local and global processes provide an
important object-association model for long observation gaps, but they do not
construct a dense scene representation.

Changing-SLAM explicitly considers both objects moving in front of the robot and
objects moved or replaced after an area has already been
mapped~\cite{soares2023changing}. It combines dynamic-keypoint filtering,
object-level tracking, a Bayesian persistence belief, and long-term association
inside ORB-SLAM3. The relevant changes are evaluated within continuous sequences,
not through a documented process-separated Session-A save and Session-B load
protocol. Its final geometric output remains an ORB-feature sparse map and its
main evaluation is camera trajectory robustness, rather than dense scene-history
accuracy.

Khronos is the closest dense single-session formulation. It factorizes
spatio-temporal metric-semantic SLAM into an active temporal window for D1 and a
global process that reconciles fragments across longer observation gaps for
D2~\cite{schmid2024khronos}. Dynamic entities are represented by trajectories and
time-indexed geometry, while ray-based evidence supports reasoning about abrupt
changes revealed during revisits. Its problem statement estimates one evolving
scene over $t=0,\ldots,T$ in a continuously running estimation problem. The paper
discusses multi-session change detection as prior work, but does not specify or
evaluate an export/import protocol through which a completed Khronos process
becomes the initial scene memory of a later independent session.

\subsection{Gaussian representations for single-session dynamics}

Gaussian representations have rapidly expanded dense SLAM. Gaussian Splatting
SLAM and SplaTAM established online camera tracking and dense mapping directly
against explicit 3D Gaussian maps~\cite{matsuki2024gaussianslam,keetha2024splatam},
but assume a static reconstructed scene. WildGS-SLAM learns per-pixel uncertainty
to downweight dynamic distractors and reconstruct a Gaussian map of the static
scene~\cite{zheng2025wildgsslam}. Gassidy detects object-level rendering-loss-flow
patterns and prunes dynamic Gaussians before camera and map
optimization~\cite{wen2025gassidy}. These systems improve robustness to dynamic
inputs, but removed entities are not retained as D1 histories.

A second Gaussian branch explicitly models continuously observed dynamics.
4D Gaussian Splatting SLAM separates static and dynamic primitives and learns
deformation fields for the latter~\cite{li2025fourdgsslam}. D4DGS-SLAM uses
long-range point tracks within one consecutive image sequence to estimate point
dynamics, visibility, and reliability~\cite{sun2025d4dgsslam}. DynaGSLAM models
and predicts moving-object Gaussians online~\cite{li2026dynagslam}, Flow4DGS-SLAM
propagates explicit Gaussian centers using scene-flow priors and models temporal
opacity and rotation~\cite{wang2026flow4dgsslam}, and RU4D-SLAM introduces
semantic-guided uncertainty and time-varying Gaussian attributes for robust 4D
reconstruction~\cite{zhao2026ru4dslam}. These are strong recent D1 methods: they
optimize motion observed in continuous sequences. Their use of terms such as
``long-term point tracking'' refers to tracks spanning frames in the current
sequence, not persistent memory across completed robot sessions.

GaME addresses the complementary D2 problem in a Gaussian
representation~\cite{yugay2026game}. It updates an online Gaussian map after the
scene changes outside the current view, adding newly supported geometry,
removing contradicted geometry, and preventing stale keyframes from corrupting
optimization. GaME receives camera poses and panoptic segmentation from external
estimators and does not retain visible moving-object trajectories. Thus it is a
strong dense D2 mapping baseline, not a complete D1+D2 SLAM system and not an
established process-separated D3 continuation method.

\subsection{Cross-session map maintenance (D3)}

D3 concerns the relationship between completed independent sessions rather than
the duration of an observation gap. Early long-term map-maintenance systems
updated geometric maps over repeated surveys. Pomerleau \etal update a LiDAR
point map, infer point-wise static/dynamic probabilities, and estimate local
point velocities across surveys spanning several months~\cite{pomerleau2014longterm}.
The representation is cluster-free, non-semantic, and point-level: it does not
preserve object identities, dense free space, or a time-indexed object history.
L\'azaro \etal explicitly support single and multiple mapping sessions by
updating 2D local point-cloud maps and pruning outdated graph nodes after loop
closures~\cite{lazaro2018efficient}. These works are important D3 predecessors,
but do not carry a dense D1+D2 scene model into every session.

Dense object-aware methods model inter-session changes more explicitly. Panoptic
Multi-TSDFs separates the scene into active and inactive volumetric submaps and
updates inactive entities as persistent, absent, or unobserved using current
surface and free-space conflicts~\cite{schmid2022panoptic}. The paper receives
poses from an external estimator and explicitly leaves short-term object tracking
for future work. POCD targets object changes between robot traversals and
maintains Gaussian--Beta beliefs over geometric change and
stationarity~\cite{qian2022pocd}. Its formulation assumes externally supplied
localization and states that explicit handling of dynamics is future work.
POV-SLAM jointly estimates robot pose and semi-static object consistency over
repeated visits, including warehouse trajectories captured months
apart~\cite{qian2023povslam}. Neither POCD nor POV-SLAM reconstructs the complete
D1 trajectories and temporal geometry occurring during every current session.

Persistent maps can also support localization or object reconstruction.
ObVi-SLAM alternates a deployment-time visual SLAM phase with long-term
persistent-object-map extraction and uses the previous deployment's object map
as a prior for the next~\cite{adkins2024obvislam}. Living Scenes jointly matches,
registers, and reconstructs corresponding objects from scans captured at
irregular times~\cite{zhu2024livingscenes}; it is not an online dynamic SLAM
system and does not maintain dense background memory. OASIS-Map associates
objects across a previous completed session and a current session and produces
static, appeared, disappeared, moved, and unknown states~\cite{oh2026oasismap}.
It builds per-session object maps using poses from a separate multi-session LiDAR
SLAM system before cross-session association. OASIS-Map remains concurrent
under-review work at the time of writing.

\subsection{Gaussian map revision across visits}

Recent Gaussian methods also address cross-time map revision. LT-Gaussian aligns
an outdated Gaussian map with a current LiDAR stream, detects emerging and
disappearing points through ICP and neighborhood tests, and uses the processed
old map as a prior for targeted optimization~\cite{cheng2025ltgaussian}. This is
a published D3 map-update component, but not a full D3 dynamic SLAM system: it
does not retain D1 histories or distinguish unobserved prior geometry from
confirmed absence.

GS-LTS compares current RGB-D observations with a rendered semantic Gaussian
reference, detects object addition, removal, or relocation, actively collects
additional views, and edits the affected map region~\cite{fu2025gslts}. The paper
explicitly limits itself to medium-term changes and excludes real-time moving
objects and human interactions. It is therefore a relevant concurrent D3 map
revision preprint, but not a system carrying D1+D2 into a new session.
GS-DIFF compares two independently reconstructed Gaussian scenes directly in
primitive space and introduces per-primitive observability to separate geometric
and appearance change~\cite{galappaththige2026gsdiff}. It is a change detector,
not a SLAM or persistent-map-update system, and should not be counted as a full
D3 solution.

\subsection{Positioning of this work}

The literature contains strong solutions on both levels of the problem. Khronos
is the clearest dense D1+D2 single-session system; recent 4D Gaussian methods
provide increasingly photorealistic D1 reconstructions, and GaME provides a
strong Gaussian D2 update mechanism. Panoptic Multi-TSDFs, POCD, POV-SLAM,
ObVi-SLAM, Living Scenes, OASIS-Map, and recent Gaussian map-update systems
provide different D3 components. What remains missing is the recurring interface
between these levels: a completed dense D1+D2 session exports persistent scene
memory, an independent new session reconciles D3 changes from that memory, and
the new session concurrently resumes the full D1+D2 mapping process before
emitting the prior for the following deployment.
