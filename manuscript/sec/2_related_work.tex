\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Dynamic reconstruction within a visit.}
Many systems reconstruct a scene while objects move in front of the camera. Co-Fusion, MID-Fusion, and EM-Fusion track
objects and reconstruct each in its own surface or volume model~\cite{runz2017cofusion,xu2019midfusion,
strecke2019emfusion}. DynaSLAM~II and DynoSAM estimate camera and object motion
jointly~\cite{bescos2021dynaslam2,morris2025dynosam}. DYNEMO-SLAM adds moving and relocated entities to a scene
graph~\cite{giberna2025dynemo}, and Lost~\&~Found reconstructs object trajectories during human--object
interaction~\cite{behrens2025lostfound}. 4DTAM, 4D Gaussian Splatting SLAM, DynaGSLAM, and 4D Primitive-Mache represent
time-varying scenes with dynamic surfaces, Gaussians, or persistent rigid
primitives~\cite{matsuki2025fourdtam,li2025fourdgsslam,li2026dynagslam,mazur2026primitive}. Dynablox detects moving
objects from points that enter observed free space~\cite{schmid2023dynablox}. These methods handle the motion that the
camera sees. Our layer uses the free-space cue of Dynablox to keep moving people out of the map and decides changes in view,
between observations, and between visits with one rule.

\paragraph{Change handling inside one representation.}
Most methods that update a map after unobserved changes build the update into their representation. For point clouds,
long-term mapping revises earlier scans as the environment changes~\cite{pomerleau2014longterm,lazaro2018efficient}.
DPG-SLAM keeps a history of added and removed points~\cite{walcott2012dynamic}, LT-Mapper separates alignment,
filtering, and change management~\cite{kim2022ltmapper}, ELite updates the ephemerality of each
point~\cite{gil2025elite}, and ProbPer-LiLo refines the map after it removes non-static
content~\cite{ali2026probperlilo}. For TSDFs, Fehr \etal compare reconstructions to find changed
regions~\cite{fehr2017tsdfchange}, and Panoptic Multi-TSDFs keeps object and background submaps that become
persistent, absent, or unobserved~\cite{schmid2022panoptic}. POCD models the change and stationarity of objects,
POV-SLAM couples object consistency with pose estimation~\cite{qian2022pocd,qian2023povslam}, and Khronos tracks
objects over a short window, associates their fragments, and dates appearance and disappearance from the rays it stores
over a session, so its map can be queried at earlier times within that session~\cite{schmid2024khronos}. For occupancy grids, OctoMap and wavemap clamp log odds so that cells can change
state~\cite{hornung2013octomap,reijgwart2023wavemap}, and RBIF updates occupancy from bundles of
rays~\cite{breitfuss2024rbif}. For 3D Gaussians, GaME adds Gaussians where readings lie in front of the rendered
surface, removes the segments that observations see through, and masks stale keyframe regions~\cite{yugay2026game}. LT-Gaussian removes disappeared Gaussians and adds new
geometry~\cite{cheng2025ltgaussian}, GaussianUpdate revises a Gaussian field continually as it retains earlier
content~\cite{zeng2025gaussianupdate}, and CL-Splats updates changed regions locally and can recover earlier
states~\cite{ackermann2025clsplats}. Each of these rules acts on its own primitives.

Khronos and GaME, the methods closest to our layer, also check observations against the map, but they check different
things. Khronos settles each object fragment against the rays of one session, thoroughly but only until the session ends:
the next visit starts from an empty map. GaME tests its Gaussians against each new keyframe and prunes those that
disagree, and after two revisits of our real room, 42\% and 61\% of the old object surface remain in its map
(\cref{tab:ablation_variants}). We do not ask whether a primitive conflicts with an observation, but when an object state holds.
Once that is known, it decides which observations still count and which elements remain, it carries over to the next
visit, and any mapper can use it.

\paragraph{Objects as the unit of change.}
Object-level reasoning ties a change to a physical object instead of to individual points or voxels. Bore \etal model
both local object motion and larger jumps between states~\cite{bore2019movable}. Changing-SLAM maintains a Bayesian
map of object persistence~\cite{soares2023changing}, LTC-Mapping accumulates evidence of visibility and
non-detection~\cite{matezbandera2022ltc}, and SuperMap maintains object occupancy, semantic confidence, and temporal
relations~\cite{zhao2026supermap}. ObVi-SLAM transfers object landmarks between sessions~\cite{adkins2024obvislam},
OASIS-Map associates objects across sessions~\cite{oh2026oasismap}, and Living Scenes matches and reconstructs objects
from scans taken at different times~\cite{zhu2024livingscenes}; RIO re-localizes object instances in changing indoor
scans~\cite{wald2019rio}. These methods associate objects across visits. Our layer decides when each object state holds,
which observations remain valid, and which faces, Gaussians, or cells of the map continue.

\paragraph{Models of temporal persistence.}
Another line of work models how long the content of a map persists. Hidden Markov models learn the transition
probabilities of occupancy cells~\cite{meyerdelius2012changinggrid}, FreMEn represents periodic
changes~\cite{krajnik2017fremen}, and Spatio-Temporal Hilbert Maps model occupancy as a continuous function of
time~\cite{senanayake2016sthm}. Perpetua keeps several hypotheses about the appearance and disappearance of
features~\cite{saavedraruiz2025perpetua}, and 3D VSG predicts how likely each object is to
change~\cite{looper2023vsg}. DAAAM adds semantic descriptions to a hierarchical 4D scene graph~\cite{gorlo2025daaam},
and LT-Mem stores current object states, session events, and long-term statistics~\cite{lee2026ltmem}. These models
predict or summarize change. From the observations of the current visit, our layer decides which earlier observations
and map elements still describe the scene, and it keeps every ended state as history.
