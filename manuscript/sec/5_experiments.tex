\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We first evaluate whether the shared updates reconstruct changing scenes accurately across three
representations while retaining valid geometry. We then examine Gaussian rendering and use ablations
to measure the effects of geometry retirement and observation reuse.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
The real room (three visits) and simulated apartment (two visits) contain changes both within and across visits.
Flat~\cite{schmid2022panoptic} (two visits) and eight rescanned 3RScan rooms~\cite{wald2019rio} evaluate
cross-visit changes. TESSE evaluates changes within a single visit using Khronos's official 4D protocol.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against GaME~\cite{yugay2026game} for Gaussian mapping of evolving scenes,
Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos} for spatio-temporal metric-semantic mapping, and
wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap} for efficient probabilistic occupancy mapping.
Panoptic Multi-TSDFs~\cite{schmid2022panoptic} provides an additional object-centric TSDF baseline.
Methods process the same posed sequences and carry maps across visits; the TESSE evaluation compares TSDF
reconstructions with Khronos.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Geometry uses surface F1 at 5\,cm before Gaussian refinement. Object F1 uses 0.5\,m matching and
duration-weighted snapshots; change F1 pools appearance/disappearance events across the chain.
3RScan averages geometry and object scores within rooms, then across rooms; events are pooled over all rooms.
Following GaME, every tenth frame of the final visit, except its first, is held out. After global refinement,
we report PSNR, MS-SSIM, and LPIPS; depth L1 uses pixels with measured depth. TESSE uses official aggregation
over map and query times.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
The shared tests use a 5\,cm surface tolerance and threshold $\ln 99$ across datasets.
Our TSDF and occupancy maps use 5\,cm cells. Controlled comparisons use annotated segmentation;
we also evaluate predicted masks.

\subsection{Unified Dynamic Mapping}
\label{sec:exp_main}

One evolving map must handle changes observed during a visit and changes discovered after returning.
The real and synthetic sequences test this joint setting; Flat and 3RScan test further cross-visit changes.
Our method improves geometry, object F1, and change F1 for all three representations on all four datasets
(\cref{tab:main}). The reconstruction therefore improves alongside the change decisions.
\Cref{fig:geometry_details} shows the removal of stale geometry in TSDF and occupancy maps.

\paragraph{TSDF and occupancy.}
In the real room, TSDF geometry F1 increases from 87.7 to 96.1 over Khronos, and occupancy geometry F1
increases from 84.5 to 91.0 over wavemap. Both representations also improve object and change F1 on all
four datasets. These results test the same geometry and observation decisions under signed-distance
fusion and probabilistic occupancy integration.

\paragraph{Temporal reconstruction.}
TESSE further tests whether the reconstructed scene is accurate over time. Its official 4D evaluation
aggregates over map and query times during a single visit
(\cref{tab:tesse}). Our TSDF improves all four F1 categories on both scenes. Change F1 increases from
43.6 to 79.0 in the office and from 36.9 to 92.7 in the apartment.

\subsection{Gaussian Reconstruction}
\label{sec:exp_gaussians}

\paragraph{Geometry and changes.}
The Gaussian map improves geometry, object F1, and change F1 on every dataset (\cref{tab:main}).
Before refinement, geometry F1 exceeds GaME by 4.9 points in the real room and 5.4 in the synthetic apartment.

\input{tables/rendering}

\paragraph{Novel-view rendering.}
Our Gaussian maps improve all four rendering metrics on every dataset (\cref{tab:rendering}), gaining
0.96--3.43\,dB over GaME. On Flat, depth L1 decreases from 1.07 to 0.47\,cm. The gains in color and depth
show that the updated geometry supports more accurate novel views after refinement. \Cref{fig:gaussian_details} shows the corresponding reduction in old-object residue and duplicated structure. We isolate the effect of old-observation constraints in the ablations below.

\paragraph{Changes within and across visits.}
\Cref{fig:evolution} follows one Gaussian reconstruction from a fixed living-room viewpoint.
Four snapshots show changes within visits and between them: the reconstruction follows the new furniture
configuration while retaining the surrounding room.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}

On Flat A$\rightarrow$B, we test the two reconstruction consequences of a change decision: retiring
outdated geometry and restricting observation contributions (\cref{tab:ablation_variants}). All variants
use the Gaussian representation and are compared with their matched Full configuration.

\input{tables/ablation}

\paragraph{Applying changes to geometry.}
Withholding geometry retirement reduces geometry F1 by 5.58 points, change F1 by 14.58 points, and PSNR by
3.84\,dB. The effect spans geometric and rendering quality: confirmed changes must be applied to the
stored reconstruction.

\paragraph{Observation reuse.}
Allowing old frames to supervise the current reconstruction without observation constraints reduces PSNR
from 27.77 to 22.69\,dB and increases depth L1 from 0.45 to 3.66\,cm. This comparison tests the observation
constraint as a whole. Discarding entire conflicting frames reaches similar quality on Flat, where the
second visit covers most of the scene. Selective reuse retains compatible measurements within old frames.

\paragraph{Parameter sensitivity.}
Decision odds of $9$, $99$, and $999$ yield 27.67--27.81\,dB PSNR, with at most one event difference.
The fixed-prior sweep gives similar reconstruction scores.

\paragraph{Predicted segmentation.}
OneFormer~\cite{jain2023oneformer} predictions retain 89.89 geometry F1 and 27.56\,dB PSNR,
while object and change F1 reflect segmentation errors (\cref{tab:ablation_variants}).

\paragraph{Computational cost.}
On Flat, mapping and refinement take 2,898\,s versus 2,647\,s for GaME on an RTX 5070 Ti with 16\,GB.
Peak memory is 6.6 versus 5.9\,GB. These measurements include the Gaussian reconstruction and its retained history.
