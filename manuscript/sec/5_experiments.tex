\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We attach the same update layer to a TSDF, a 3D Gaussian, and an occupancy mapper. With every mapper, the layer detects
the changes within and between runs, removes obsolete surfaces, reaches a higher geometry F1 on the revisits than
the published update of its representation, and completes the map from the memory of earlier runs. Each mapper also runs without our
layer, from an empty map and with its own update where it has one.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
\emph{Real room.} We record one room three times with a Kinect: run A (133\,s, 4{,}003 frames), run B (135\,s,
4{,}041 frames) about six hours later, and run C (241\,s, 7{,}216 frames) twelve days after B. B starts from A's
exported state and C from B's. Camera poses come from ORB-SLAM3~\cite{campos2021orbslam3}, and we align B and C with A by
rigid transforms set manually in Open3D~\cite{zhou2018open3d} to minimize the alignment error between the meshes. We call
every run after the first a revisit. The room contains 20 objects. Between A and B,
seven moved objects, one addition, and one removal give 16 appearance or disappearance events, and between B and C,
four moved objects and two removals give 10. During each run, an object is also placed or moved while the camera maps
another part of the room.
Object reference surfaces come from RGB-D measurements aggregated over annotated state intervals.
\emph{Simulated apartment.} Two Isaac Sim runs observe one furnished apartment: A with 4{,}186 frames and B with
3{,}555 frames at 30\,Hz, with exact poses. B starts from A's exported state and
observes only part of what A mapped. Run A contains 26 appearance or disappearance events and run B 14, in view or out of view,
and 32 events happen between the runs. The complete scene mesh is the reference inside and outside the current camera
coverage.
\emph{Flat.} The public Flat dataset~\cite{schmid2022panoptic}, which Panoptic Multi-TSDFs and GaME also use,
has two simulated runs of one flat with 400 and 362 frames and exact poses. Between the runs, objects
move, appear, and disappear. A point reference of each run's scene comes with the data.

\paragraph{Compared methods.}
We compare against published methods on the same recordings. Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos} maps each run as its own
session with dynamic and change reasoning. Panoptic Multi-TSDFs~\cite{schmid2022panoptic} keeps object submaps that
become persistent, absent, or unobserved across runs. GaME~\cite{yugay2026game} updates a Gaussian map after changes out
of view, and wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap} lowers the occupancy of the cells that rays traverse. We run our layer on
three backends: a Khronos TSDF pipeline, GaME, and wavemap. Each backend keeps its inputs and resolution, and our rows
connect it to the layer through the update interface of \cref{sec:representations}.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
\emph{Khronos' object F1} scores the objects of a TSDF map at every moment against the objects present at that
moment, weighted by the duration of each state, with the object evaluator released with Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos}: objects
are associated by surface, must share the class, and match within 2\,m.
% PENDING 4D object F1: per object and moment, the map surface of the object against its true surface at 5 cm.
\emph{Change F1} is the change metric of Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos}. A moved object gives two events, the
disappearance of its old state and the appearance of its new one, and a change of the map that matches no reference event
is a false positive. A state is represented in a map when at least half of its reference surface samples lie within
10\,cm of the predicted surface, of the same class where the map carries object labels, and an event is detected when the
map no longer represents a state that disappeared or newly represents one that appeared. We report changes within a run,
which happen in view or out of view, and changes between runs.
\emph{Old-site residue} is the fraction of the reference surface of object states that ended before a run began,
among the parts that the run observes as free space, that the run's final map still shows. Lower is better.
\emph{Geometry} is the F1 at 5\,cm between surface samples of the map and the reference. The real reference is
reconstructed from each run's own RGB-D data and covers what that run observes, so it tests accuracy there. The
simulated reference is the complete scene mesh, including surfaces that the last run leaves unobserved, so it also
tests completeness. We score each map on its own surface: the TSDF mesh,
the occupied cells, and a TSDF fused from depth rendered from the Gaussians~\cite{huang20242dgs}.
\emph{Rendering} of the Gaussian maps is evaluated, as in GaME~\cite{yugay2026game}, on every tenth frame of the last
run, held out from mapping, with PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018lpips}, and depth L1 as GaME's evaluation computes them, SSIM
in its multi-scale form~\cite{wang2003msssim}, after the refinement that follows the last run. \emph{Own time}
renders each frame with the map of its own timestamp, $\mathcal{M}(t_k)$.
In the tables, n/a marks a quantity the method does not produce: Khronos maps each run as its own session, and only
Gaussian maps render. A dash marks a quantity not evaluated.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Simulated runs use ground-truth poses, and real runs use the estimated poses and the alignment above. Segmentation is
ground truth in all runs, annotated on SAM~\cite{kirillov2023sam} masks for the real room. Datasets and backends share
the constants of \cref{sec:handoff}. Every experiment runs on one NVIDIA RTX 5070 Ti (16\,GB) and an AMD Ryzen 7 9700X with 44\,GB of memory.

\paragraph{With our layer, every map detects at least 90\% of the changes within and between runs, without a false
detection.}
Within a run, all three backends reach change F1 100 in every real run and 100 and 96.3 in the simulated runs A and B. Khronos reaches 66.7 in the real runs B and C and GaME 66.7 in run B, and the
compared methods reach 67.9 to 91.7 on the simulated runs. Between runs, the TSDF and Gaussian backends detect 15 of the
16 events from A to B and the occupancy backend all 16. Every backend detects all 10 events from B to C and 29 of the
32 on the simulated apartment (\cref{tab:main_real,tab:main_sim}). On these three transitions, Panoptic Multi-TSDFs
reaches change F1 64.0, 37.5, and 91.8, GaME 93.3, 94.7, and 81.5, and wavemap 93.3, 100, and 93.3.

\paragraph{Obsolete surfaces leave the map.}
After the revisits of the real room, 0.9\% and 7.0\% of the old object surface remain in the TSDF and 8.1\% and 14.3\%
in the Gaussian map, against 57.1\% and 60.1\% for Panoptic Multi-TSDFs and 42.4\% and 61.1\% under GaME's update
(\cref{tab:main_real}). Without change handling, an occupancy map keeps 99.6\% of the old surface, and with our layer
5.8\% and 12.2\% remain, at geometry F1 92.0 and 91.6 (\cref{tab:ablation_variants}). Wavemap's update, which lowers the
occupancy of every cell that a ray traverses, leaves 1.3\% and 4.4\% at 88.9 and 89.0.

\paragraph{On the revisits, every map reaches a higher geometry F1 than the published update of its representation.}
On the real room, with camera poses estimated by ORB-SLAM3, our geometry F1 in runs B and C is 96.0 and 95.3 with the
TSDF (Panoptic Multi-TSDFs: 93.6 and 93.0), 93.4 and 95.0 with the Gaussians (GaME: 88.6 and 90.8), and 92.0 and 91.6
with occupancy (wavemap: 88.9 and 89.0) (\cref{tab:main_real}). The TSDF map also exceeds Khronos, which starts each
run from an empty map, on every revisit (\cref{tab:main_real,tab:main_sim,tab:flat}).

\paragraph{The memory of earlier runs completes every map.}
On the complete simulated scene, the updated maps of run B reach geometry F1 98.5 (TSDF), 96.6 (Gaussians), and 94.9
(occupancy). Maps of run B alone reach 83.6, 85.4, and 84.2, and the compared methods that carry a map reach 92.1
(Panoptic Multi-TSDFs), 92.0 (GaME), and 91.2 (wavemap) (\cref{tab:main_sim,tab:ablation_variants}).

Our layer also renders better than GaME's update (\cref{tab:rendering}). On held-out frames of the last run,
our final map renders 2.81\,dB better than GaME's update on the simulated apartment, 0.96\,dB better on the real room,
and 1.47\,dB better on Flat, and it is better on every metric. GaME's update renders 1.27 and 0.99\,dB below a map rebuilt from scratch, whereas our
final map renders 1.54\,dB above it on the simulated apartment and within 0.03\,dB of it on the real room while keeping the earlier runs. Because objects
also change during the last run,
rendering each frame with our map of its own time raises PSNR further, by 1.64 and 0.51\,dB on the simulated apartment and
the real room, and improves every metric of our final map. GaME keeps only its final map. The refinement lowers GaME's geometry F1 from 87.7 to 84.6 on Flat and
from 90.8 to 89.2 on the real room and changes ours by at most 0.12 points.

\paragraph{A restarted map is incomplete, and a carried-over map is stale.}
Maps rebuilt from scratch reach geometry F1 83.6 to 85.4 against the complete simulated scene, and a carried-over
occupancy map keeps 99.6\% of the old object surface, with real geometry F1 85.2 and 82.2 (\cref{tab:ablation_variants}).
GaME's update keeps 42.4\% and 61.1\% of the old surface. With our layer, every backend reaches its highest geometry F1
against the complete simulated scene.

\paragraph{Valid domains add accuracy to the Gaussian map.}
Letting each keyframe train only the Gaussians in its valid domain, instead of every stored keyframe training the
current map, raises the geometry F1 of real run B by 3.1 points (supplementary material).
