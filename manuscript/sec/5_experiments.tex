\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate whether carrying scene state across deployments preserves useful
geometry, removes obsolete object states, and continues to support dynamic
mapping in the new run. We use repeated real-room recordings, controlled
synthetic data with exact ground truth, and public RGB-D sequences.

\subsection{Datasets and Baselines}
\label{sec:datasets}

\paragraph{Repeated real-room deployments.}
We use three RGB-D deployments, A, B, and C, recorded in the same occupied room
on three days. The data contain 20 physical object identities, changes within
each deployment, and 16 annotated changed states between A and B. Object
reference surfaces are aggregated from depth during manually annotated presence
intervals, using 2\,cm voxels observed in at least three frames. Scene geometry
is evaluated against a 1\,cm reconstruction from the measurements of each
deployment. B and C are used for evaluation.

\paragraph{Controlled synthetic deployments.}
Two Isaac Sim sequences revisit a furnished apartment with scripted visible
motion, changes while the camera looks elsewhere, and changes between the end
of A and the start of B. They contain 4,186 and 4,041 frames at 30\,Hz.
Per-frame object poses, visibility, and the scene mesh are known exactly. The
annotations contain 24 hidden within-deployment changes in A, 10 in B, and 32
cross-deployment changes.

\paragraph{Public sequences.}
We use three 3RScan reference/rescan pairs~\cite{wald2019rio}: an unchanged
scene, a scene with two moved chairs and one removed chair, and a scene with a
non-rigid blanket change. We initialize the map from the reference scan and load
it for the rescan. Bonn RGB-D Dynamic~\cite{palazzolo2019bonn} provides three
short box-manipulation sequences, and TUM~\cite{sturm2012tum} provides dynamic
person sequences.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare with the official Khronos release~\cite{schmid2024khronos} and
Panoptic Multi-TSDFs~\cite{schmid2022panoptic}. Khronos starts each deployment
from an empty map. Panoptic loads the previous map using the same
inter-deployment transform as our method. \emph{Ours, from scratch} runs our
method without importing previous-deployment state.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:protocols}

\paragraph{Object reconstruction.}
We report duration-weighted object precision, recall, and F1 over each
deployment using the Khronos object evaluator with a 2\,m association gate.

\paragraph{Changed object states.}
For an annotated object-state surface $G$ and predicted map $M$, we measure the
fraction of the state represented by same-class predicted surface within
10\,cm,
\begin{equation}
 r(G,M)=\frac{|\{x\in G:d(x,M_{\ell(G)})\le0.10\,\mathrm{m}\}|}{|G|}.
 \label{eq:representedness}
\end{equation}
A state is represented when $r(G,M)\ge0.5$. A moved object contributes the
disappearance of its old state and the appearance of its new state.
Precision, recall, and F1 are computed over these appearance and disappearance
events. \emph{Old-location residue} measures how much surface from a departed
state remains in the final map.

\paragraph{Scene geometry.}
Geometry precision, recall, and F1 compare predicted and reference surfaces at
5, 10, and 20\,cm; the tables report 5\,cm. Real-room results use a room crop,
synthetic results use the simulator mesh, and 3RScan uses the rescan
reconstruction. For TUM, we additionally measure the fraction of final-map
vertices that agree in depth with person pixels in at least three sampled
frames.

\subsection{Repeated Real-Room Mapping}

Carrying scene state improves object reconstruction in the later deployments.
Object F1 rises from 94.6 to 98.7 in B and from 96.4 to 98.6 in C relative to
our from-scratch runs. Compared with Khronos, the corresponding object F1 gains
are 7.2 and 14.8 percentage points. Hidden-change F1 is 100.0 in B and C,
compared with 66.7 for Khronos.

Across deployments, our method detects 15 of 16 changed states from A to B and
all annotated changes from B to C. Old-location residue is 6.8\% and 10.9\%,
compared with 57.1\% and 60.1\% for Panoptic. The retained map also has higher
geometry recall than the from-scratch variants, at the cost of lower precision
because old and newly reconstructed background surfaces can both remain when
the two deployments are slightly misaligned.

With exact ground truth, our method detects all 24 hidden changes in A and all

On the unchanged 3RScan scene, our method produces no false change. In Scene 2,
both moved chairs are represented at their new positions and all five unchanged
objects are retained; the removed chair is missed. Scene 3 produces one missed
and one spurious event. Geometry F1 is higher than Khronos in all three rescans.

Bonn and TUM test behavior during a single run. In Bonn's placing sequence, the
final box state has represented-surface fractions of 1.00 for our method and
0.43 for Khronos. In the moving sequence, both systems retain the first rest
state because the observed 0.75\,m motion is below the 1\,m displacement
threshold inherited from Khronos. On TUM \emph{walking\_xyz}, person
contamination is 1.02\% for our method and 1.09\% for Khronos, while our map
retains 29\% more static surface.
