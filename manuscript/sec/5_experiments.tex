\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We attach the same update layer to a TSDF, a 3D Gaussian, and an occupancy mapper. With every mapper, the layer detects
the changes within and between visits, removes obsolete surfaces, reaches a higher geometry F1 on the revisits than
the published update of its representation, and completes the map from the memory of earlier visits. Each mapper also runs without our
layer, from an empty map and with its own update where it has one.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
\emph{Real room.} We record one room three times with a Kinect: the first visit has 4{,}003 frames (133\,s), the second
4{,}041 (135\,s), and the third 7{,}216 (241\,s). Each visit starts from the state that the previous one exported. Camera
poses come from ORB-SLAM3~\cite{campos2021orbslam3}, and we align the second and third visits with the first by
rigid transforms set manually in Open3D~\cite{zhou2018open3d} to minimize the alignment error between the meshes. We call
every visit after the first a revisit. The room contains 20 objects. Between the first and second
visits, eight moved objects, one addition, and one removal give 18 appearance or disappearance events, and between the
second and third, five moved objects and two removals give 12. During each visit, an object is also placed or moved while the camera maps
another part of the room.
Object reference surfaces come from RGB-D measurements aggregated over annotated state intervals.
\emph{Simulated apartment.} Two visits in Isaac Sim observe one furnished apartment, with 4{,}186 and 3{,}555 frames at
30\,Hz and exact poses. The second visit starts from the state that the first exported and observes only part of what
the first mapped. The first visit contains 26 appearance or disappearance events and the second 14, in view or out of view,
and 32 events happen between the visits. The complete scene mesh is the reference inside and outside the current camera
coverage.
\emph{Flat.} The public Flat dataset~\cite{schmid2022panoptic}, which Panoptic Multi-TSDFs and GaME also use,
has two simulated visits of one flat with 400 and 362 frames and exact poses. Between the visits, objects
move, appear, and disappear. A point reference of each visit's scene comes with the data.
\emph{TESSE.} The TESSE change-detection data released with Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos} has two simulated scenes,
each one visit with exact poses: an office of 4{,}346 frames (217\,s), during which five objects disappear and three
appear, and an apartment of 1{,}745 frames (87\,s), during which four disappear and two appear. Khronos and our TSDF
backend map both with Khronos' default depth range of 5\,m.
\emph{3RScan.} Indoor rooms scanned repeatedly months apart, with the changed objects
annotated~\cite{wald2019rio}. We take 8 rooms with three or four scans each (27 visits) in which every rescan moves an
object by at least 0.3\,m or removes one. Each scan is a short RGB-D sequence, aligned to the first scan of its room by
the transforms of the dataset. The reference holds every annotated object, with one state per position and each change
at the start of the scan that shows it.

\paragraph{Compared methods.}
We compare against published methods on the same recordings. Every method maps the visits of a dataset in order and
carries its map from one visit to the next. Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos}, which reasons about dynamic objects and
changes within a session, maps the visits as one sequence. Panoptic Multi-TSDFs~\cite{schmid2022panoptic} keeps object submaps that
become persistent, absent, or unobserved across visits. GaME~\cite{yugay2026game} updates a Gaussian map after changes out
of view, and wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap} lowers the occupancy of the cells that rays traverse. We run our layer on
three backends: a Khronos TSDF pipeline, GaME, and wavemap. Each backend keeps its inputs and resolution, and our rows
connect it to the layer through the update interface of \cref{sec:representations}.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
% PENDING 4D object F1: per object and moment, the map surface of the object against its true surface at 5 cm.
\emph{Change F1} is the change metric of Khronos~\cite{schmid2024khronos}. A moved object gives two events, the
disappearance of its old state and the appearance of its new one, and a change of the map that matches no reference event
is a false positive. A state is represented in a map when at least half of its reference surface samples lie within
10\,cm of the predicted surface, of the same class where the map carries object labels, and an event is detected when the
map no longer represents a state that disappeared or newly represents one that appeared. The main results count all
events of a dataset, within and between visits, and the supplementary material reports them per visit. On TESSE and
3RScan, we use Khronos' 4D metric: each score is averaged over the times at which a map is saved and the earlier times
about which it is queried, with the background scored at 20\,cm as in Khronos. Its change F1 counts the objects that
are associated with a reference object; the inclusive change F1 also counts the missed and the spurious ones.
\emph{Object F1} is the online object F1 of Khronos: at every moment, the objects of the map are matched to the objects
present then, by surface, of the same class and within 2\,m, and the F1 is averaged over the visit with the duration of
each map state as weight.
\emph{Old-site residue} is the fraction of the reference surface of object states that ended before a visit began,
among the parts that the visit observes as free space, that the visit's final map still shows. Lower is better.
\emph{Geometry} is the F1 at 5\,cm between surface samples of the map and the reference. The real reference is
reconstructed from each visit's own RGB-D data and covers what that visit observes, so it tests accuracy there. The
simulated reference is the complete scene mesh, including surfaces that the last visit leaves unobserved, so it also
tests completeness. We score each map on its own surface: the TSDF mesh,
the occupied cells, and a TSDF fused from depth rendered from the Gaussians~\cite{huang20242dgs}.
\emph{Rendering} of the Gaussian maps is evaluated, as in GaME~\cite{yugay2026game}, on every tenth frame of the last
visit, held out from mapping, with PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018lpips}, and depth L1 as GaME's evaluation computes them, SSIM
in its multi-scale form~\cite{wang2003msssim}, after the refinement that follows the last visit.
In the tables, n/a marks a quantity the method does not produce: only Gaussian maps render, and Khronos run per visit
carries no map between visits. A dash marks a quantity not evaluated.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Simulated visits use ground-truth poses, and real visits use the estimated poses and the alignment above. Segmentation is
ground truth in all visits, annotated on SAM~\cite{kirillov2023sam} masks for the real room. Our layer and Panoptic
Multi-TSDFs receive the object instances. Khronos receives the class labels and forms objects with its own segmentation,
as in its release; on the simulated apartment, it receives the same instances.

\paragraph{Obsolete surfaces leave the map.}
After the revisits of the real room, 0.9\% and 6.9\% of the old object surface remain in the TSDF and 8.1\% and 14.3\%
in the Gaussian map, against 57.1\% and 60.1\% for Panoptic Multi-TSDFs and 42.4\% and 61.1\% under GaME's update
(\cref{tab:ablation_variants,fig:qualitative}). With our layer, 5.8\% and 12.2\% remain in the occupancy map, at geometry
F1 92.0 and 91.6 (\cref{tab:ablation_variants}). Wavemap's update, which lowers the
occupancy of every cell that a ray traverses, leaves 1.3\% and 4.4\% at 88.9 and 89.0.
\paragraph{On the revisits, every map reaches a higher geometry F1 than the published update of its representation.}
On the real room, with camera poses estimated by ORB-SLAM3, our geometry F1 in the second and third visits is 96.0 and 94.6 with the
TSDF (Panoptic Multi-TSDFs: 93.6 and 93.0), 93.4 and 95.0 with the Gaussians (GaME: 88.6 and 90.8), and 92.0 and 91.6
with occupancy (wavemap: 88.9 and 89.0) (\cref{tab:main}; per visit in the supplementary material). The TSDF map also exceeds Khronos, run over the
visits as one sequence, on every revisit, and it leads Khronos on Khronos' own object F1 in every visit (supplementary
material).

\paragraph{The memory of earlier visits completes every map.}
On the complete simulated scene, the updated maps of the second visit reach geometry F1 99.2 (TSDF), 96.7 (Gaussians), and 94.9
(occupancy). Maps of the second visit alone reach 83.6, 85.4, and 84.2, and the compared methods that carry a map reach 92.1
(Panoptic Multi-TSDFs), 92.0 (GaME), and 91.2 (wavemap) (\cref{tab:ablation_variants}).

\paragraph{On Flat, the methods differ only after the scene has changed.}
In the first visit, which contains no changes, every evaluated method starts from an empty map. The geometry F1 of
GaME and our maps agrees within 0.2 points (86.3 to 86.5), and Khronos and Panoptic Multi-TSDFs reach 86.0 and 85.1
(supplementary material). In the second visit, our layer reaches 90.2 with the TSDF, against 86.9 for Khronos, which
maps both visits as one sequence, and 88.9 for Panoptic Multi-TSDFs. With the Gaussians, it reaches 89.8, against 87.7 for
GaME, which also keeps the first visit's map, and with occupancy \prov{80.3}, against 78.6 for wavemap.

\paragraph{A restarted map is incomplete.}
Maps rebuilt from scratch hold only the current visit. Averaged over the visits, they reach geometry F1 of 87.2 to 90.2
against the complete simulated scene, where our maps reach 94.7 to 99.2. On the real room, whose reference is
reconstructed from each visit's own frames and so suits a map built from that visit alone, they reach 93.9 to 95.7, and
our maps, which keep the earlier visits, reach 92.1 to 95.0 (\cref{tab:ablation_variants,fig:problem}). GaME's update
keeps 42.4\% and 61.1\% of the old surface. With our layer, every backend reaches its highest geometry F1
against the complete simulated scene.

\paragraph{Valid domains add accuracy to the Gaussian map.}
Letting each keyframe train only the Gaussians in its valid domain, instead of every stored keyframe training the
current map, raises the geometry F1 of the second real visit by 3.1 points (supplementary material).
