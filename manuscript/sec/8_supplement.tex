\section{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:supp_impl}

\subsection{State Intervals}
\label{sec:supp_states}

To test a state, the layer projects up to 1{,}500 surface samples of the state, placed on a 2.5\,cm grid, into each
later depth image. A pixel without depth or outside the image gives no evidence, and readings more than $60^\circ$ from
the sample normal do not count~\cite{nguyen2012kinect}. Thin parts, edges, and mislabeled pixels produce false
see-through readings. A sample is reliable if its posterior of belonging to the object is at least one half under a
two-component label model, fitted by expectation maximization at every round, and if it was never seen through while
the object was confirmed in place. The Beta density $p_A$ of the absence statistic $L_t$ shrinks toward pooled
sensor statistics, and a depth image contributes only if it judges at least $\min(30, \nu_{\mathrm{rel}})$ samples.

For the displacement factor, $\theta \sim \mathrm{Beta}(1/2 + n_{\mathrm{eff}}\phi,\ 1/2 + n_{\mathrm{eff}}(1-\phi))$, with
$\phi$ the fraction of $N$'s surface cells that lie farther than 10\,cm from $G_A$ and $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ their effective
number. The
pseudo-counts $\alpha_c, \beta_c$ of the prior $q_\ell$ come from the other objects of the same class and group, and the class mean starts
from Jeffreys' reference value $\frac{1}{2}$, or from $\frac{3}{4}$ and $\frac{1}{4}$ for classes declared movable or
fixed. Every action in a change-detection round reads $q$ from the history before that round, so the decisions are
independent of the identifiers, their order, and the end of a visit.

A state $A$ ends without a successor when $\max(\operatorname{logit} q + L^{\mathrm{pre}}_t,\ L_t) > \ln 99$ and the
same images see through $G_A$ more often than they support it. The prior term applies only to a state inherited from the
previous visit. The observations $N$ merge into $A$ when $A$ has current support and
$1 - q \ge q\,B_{M:S}$. The map shows $G_A \cup G_N$ while $S$ is the most probable explanation and $G_A$ otherwise,
until a handover commits. At the end of a visit, the same rules apply to the evidence gathered so far, and a remaining
$N$ that $M$ explains best goes to history.

\subsection{Backends}
\label{sec:supp_backends}

\paragraph{TSDF.}
When a round changes the state intervals, the backend rebuilds the affected blocks by integrating their frames again in
their original order, which keeps the weight saturation of the native rule. Each face takes the identity of the nearest
current object face within one voxel and is background otherwise. Where the visit's frames leave gaps in the present
surface, the backend fills them with the background faces of the active window, which it archives at every round. Fill
faces and the background faces of the previous visit's shown map pass the continuation test, the former with
$K_{\mathrm{prior}} = 0$ and the latter with $K_{\mathrm{prior}} = 1$. Background faces of the previous map leave
together with a closed object state whose old site they cover, and fill faces measured no later than that state's last
support leave as well. Object faces follow their state. A face of a continuing inherited state also leaves if it lies
inside the object's present surface, which the backend tests by casting rays in 64 fixed directions and comparing hits
on the inner and outer sides. The end of a visit only drains the queues and saves the state, so the final map is the
online map.

\paragraph{Gaussians.}
FlashSplat~\cite{shen2024flashsplat} assigns identities to the Gaussians, accumulated over keyframes. The continuation
test runs at each keyframe of the current visit, before GaME adds new Gaussians, with $h_e$ three standard deviations and
$\tau_{\min} = 5$\,cm. A reading covers a Gaussian only if it comes from the side from which the
previous visit saw the Gaussian. A Gaussian that the visit supports or covers is \emph{present} and is trained by the
current visit's keyframes; one that it supports but never covers is also \emph{shared} and is trained by the keyframes of
both visits; every other Gaussian is \emph{held}: the current visit leaves it unchanged, and the keyframes of its own visit
keep maintaining it. Each optimization step draws one keyframe. A keyframe of the current visit has weight one. An
earlier keyframe has as weight the fraction of the Gaussians it shows, among those the current visit has decided on, that
are shared and that the current visit has observed less than a single earlier view does: their observed area $(f/z)^2$,
summed over the views of the current visit, stays below its mean over the earlier views. The earlier views of one place
thus share that place's budget, and a distant new view does not replace close earlier detail. A step updates only the
Gaussians its keyframe may train, and its loss counts only the pixels whose first Gaussian, where the transmittance
along the ray first drops below one half, is one of them. GaME's opacity pruning removes only Gaussians created in the
current visit. The refinement after the last visit follows the same rules and adds no Gaussians. \Cref{sec:supp_gaussian}
switches these choices off one at a time.

\section{Refinement after the Last Visit}
\label{sec:supp_refinement}

The refinement that follows the last visit applies the mapping's rules: each element is trained only by the keyframes that
may train it during mapping, ended elements stay fixed, and the refinement adds no elements. It raises PSNR on all three
datasets, by 3.85, 1.74, and 0.93\,dB, and changes geometry by at most 0.12 points (\cref{tab:supp_refinement}). GaME
refines its final map on its keyframes outside their stale masks, and its geometry falls on all three datasets.

\section{Runtime}
\label{sec:supp_runtime}

All timings use one NVIDIA RTX 5070 Ti (16\,GB) and an AMD Ryzen 7 9700X. We give mapping speed as the frames of a visit
per second of mapping, which excludes exporting the map. On the three real
visits, the TSDF backend maps \prov{3.1}, \prov{3.5}, and \prov{2.1} frames per second, the Gaussian backend 3.8, 2.4, and 1.4 with a peak GPU
memory of at most 8.3\,GB, and the occupancy backend 8.9, 6.9, and 5.6.
