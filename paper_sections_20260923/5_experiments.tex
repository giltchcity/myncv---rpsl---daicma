\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

All numbers in this section are produced by one evaluation entry point from
result files (nothing typed in) for one code version; run-to-run noise measured
on identical-behaviour runs is $\pm0.1$--$0.2$\,pp for the real online object
score and $\pm0.1$\,pp for real geometry; event counts are deterministic.

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}

\paragraph{Synthetic A$\rightarrow$B (Isaac Sim).}
Two sessions of a furnished apartment with scripted object animations: motion
in view (D1), hidden moves and removals while the camera looks elsewhere (D2),
and 32 cross-session changes between the end of A and the start of B (D3).
Ground truth is exact: per-frame object poses and visibility, and the true
scene mesh. 4186 and 4041 frames at 30\,Hz.

\paragraph{Real room-18 A/B/C.}
Three deployments of a hand-held RGB-D camera in an occupied room on three
days, with objects moved, removed and added between and within sessions
(20 physical identities, 16 D3 changes, hidden D2 moves in every session).
Object states are human-annotated as surfaces aggregated from the sensor's own
depth over the annotated presence interval (2\,cm voxels seen in $\ge 3$
frames); geometry is scored against a 1\,cm same-sensor reconstruction of each
session. B and C were never used for development.

\paragraph{3RScan~\cite{wald2019rio}.}
Reference/rescan pairs of the same room scanned at different times: pure D3
(each scan is static), with per-object rigid-motion, removal and non-rigid
annotations and dense instance labels. Depth is $224\times172$ at 10\,Hz. We
use three train/val scenes: a static control, a scene with two moved chairs and
one removed chair, and a scene with a non-rigid blanket. We treat the reference
as session A (from empty) and the rescan as session B loading A. We note that
3RScan's \emph{rigid} list contains near-identity alignments; the true motion
is the composition of the scan transform and the instance transform, and a
listed object whose reference pose is still represented by its rescan pose is
static.

\paragraph{Bonn RGB-D Dynamic~\cite{palazzolo2019bonn} and TUM~\cite{sturm2012tum}.}
Single 15--25\,s sequences with a box moved, placed or removed in view (D1), and
two sequences with walking people. They have no object ground truth; we use
them to check that adding memory does not hurt single-session behaviour.

\subsection{Protocols}
\label{sec:protocols}

\emph{Object} is the official Khronos object evaluator (duration-weighted,
non-reviewed corrected, 2\,m surface association) on the online map.
\emph{Change D2/D3} follows protocol\,v1: a state is represented when $\ge50\%$
of its ground-truth surface lies within 10\,cm of a same-class predicted
surface; D2 compares the map before and after the in-session anchor time, D3
compares the inherited state with the end of the next session; P/R/F1 count
appeared and disappeared events. \emph{Old-site residue} is the share of the
ground-truth surface of a departed state that the final map still shows.
\emph{G1} is a plain two-mesh F-score at 5/10/20\,cm inside an oriented room
crop against the 1\,cm same-sensor reconstruction of the session (precision
therefore counts remembered surface the session did not re-observe as error);
\emph{G2} restricts the score to the observed domain. On 3RScan, states are the
annotated-mesh vertices per object id (identity exact), moved objects are judged
on the discriminative part of their surface, and only objects visible for
$\ge 2$\,s of frames are evaluated; geometry is a two-mesh F-score against the
scan's own reconstruction mesh. On Bonn the box's rest poses are built from the
dataset itself (3D-consistent instance masks, depth and ground-truth poses) and
tested with the same representedness rule; on TUM we report the share of final
map vertices that project onto person pixels with agreeing depth in $\ge 3$
sampled frames.

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

\emph{Khronos}~\cite{schmid2024khronos}: the official release, every session
from scratch (its released workflow has no session import); on cross-session
protocols its ``disappeared'' events therefore come for free.
\emph{Panoptic Multi-TSDFs}~\cite{schmid2022panoptic}: the official release,
run multi-session by loading the previous map with the same rigid transform we
use; on 3RScan with its own 3RScan configuration.
\emph{Ours from scratch}: our system without imported state, the memory
ablation.

\paragraph{Real data (\cref{tab:real,tab:real_d3}).}
Memory raises the online object score of every later session (B $+4.1$, C
$+2.2$\,pp over our own from-scratch run; $+7.2$ and $+14.8$ over Khronos) and
keeps hidden in-session changes at F1\,100 where Khronos reaches 66.7. Across
sessions, 15 of 16 changed states are reconciled in A$\rightarrow$B (the
remaining one is a swivel chair that a person moved within A, annotated as
static) and all in B$\rightarrow$C, against 64.0 and 37.5 for Panoptic, and the
surface still shown at departed sites falls to 6.8\,\% and 10.9\,\% (Panoptic
57--60\,\%). Geometry shows the price of memory: recall is highest with memory
(98.4/98.9), but precision in the room crop is 6--8\,pp below the from-scratch
map because inherited surface that the session did not re-observe is counted
as error by a session-only reference, and because a re-observed wall carried
from the previous session stays next to the session's own copy when the two
sessions' drifts differ by more than the 5\,cm tolerance (\cref{sec:limitations}).
Under the observed-domain score G2 the gap is 2.6--3.3\,pp.

\paragraph{3RScan (\cref{tab:3rscan}).}
On the public multi-scan data the ordering is the same as on our own: in the
scene with real changes we reconcile both moved chairs (old state ended, new
state built), keep all five static objects and miss only the removed chair,
whose site the 39\,s rescan sees empty on 71\,\% of its reliable surface---not
enough for the accumulation of \cref{sec:absence}. Panoptic, with its own 3RScan
configuration, misses the new pose of the turned desk chair and reports the
monitor absent; Khronos rebuilt from scratch obtains its three ``disappeared''
events for free and reports static objects absent whenever a short scan does
not rebuild them (three in the static control, five in scene\,3). Memory again
trades precision for recall: the chained map reaches the higher F-score in all
three rescans (recall $+6$ to $+23$\,pp, precision $-2$ to $-8$\,pp).

\paragraph{Bonn and TUM.}
Where there is nothing to remember, memory must not hurt. On the three Bonn box
sequences our maps match Khronos: the placed box is reconstructed at rest
(ours 1.00 represented, Khronos 0.43), the removing sequence ends one second
after the pick-up and is undecidable for both, and in the moving sequence both
systems keep the box's first rest pose---the box moved $0.75$\,m in view, below
the $1$\,m displacement Khronos' extractor requires to accept observed motion,
which we inherit unchanged. On TUM \emph{walking\_xyz} the walking people
contaminate 1.02\,\% of our final map and 1.09\,\% of Khronos', while our map
retains 29\,\% more static surface.

\paragraph{Evaluator note.}
The published object protocol associates predictions to ground truth within
2\,m. A stale old state 1--2\,m from the object's current position is thereby
credited as a hit until it is cleared; correct early clearing is penalised
until the new state is reconstructed. With the evaluator's default 0.5\,m gate
both our previous and current versions lose $0.9$\,pp on real B and keep their
order; we report the published protocol throughout.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Three limitations are visible in the numbers. (1) Removed objects: the old site
of an object that has left is cleared through the look-wise accumulation, which
is slow when the site is seen only partially (real C residue 10.9\,\%, the
3RScan chair). (2) Carried geometry: a wall re-observed by the session is kept
next to the session's copy when the two sessions' drifts differ by more than the
sensor tolerance---two thirds of the carried false surface on real C---because
inherited geometry is merged as a mesh, not fused as evidence. (3) Observed
motion smaller than the extractor's displacement gate is inherited from the base
system. All three are addressed by the same principle the method already uses
at the fragment level: treating every remembered surface cell as evidence with a
belief, updated by the sensor model, instead of a rule per case.
